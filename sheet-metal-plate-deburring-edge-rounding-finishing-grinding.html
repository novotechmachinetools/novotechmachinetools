Metal deburring and finishing machines, 5-7/8″ to 53-1/8″ wide

A metal deburring machine takes the burr, slag and sharp edges off laser-, punch- and plasma-cut parts and gives them an even finish — in one pass on a conveyor, where hand grinding takes minutes per part and never comes out the same twice. NovoTech sells the machines of Gecam, built in Parma, Italy, small to large: wide-belt deburring and satin-finishing machines from the 5-7/8″ G15 to the 53-1/8″ G135, dry or wet, with an abrasive belt, top brushes and Scotch-Brite brushes in one pass; edge rounding machines that round every edge to 5/64″ (2 mm), inside holes too, or both sides of the part at once; the G65DCC, which breaks the slag off thick plasma and oxy-fuel parts; and, for shops that do not need a wide-belt line, CUB brush machines, the EASY T brushing workstation and GCB crossed-belt benches for heavy, long and shaped parts. Supplied with a CSA-approved panel, installed and supported from Mississauga, ON, Canada. For the shop that starts deburring by machine, the NovoTech value-line MGM-300 (12″) and BFM-2560 (24″, with a wet dust collector) are wide-belt machines for laser-cut and punched parts.

  • Wide-belt deburring and satin finishing, 5-7/8″ to 53-1/8″ wide · parts .020″ to 4-3/4″ thick, dry or wet
  • Edge rounding to 5/64″ (2 mm), both sides in one pass on the GT06 and GT13 · G65DCC deslagging for plasma parts 3/8″ – 4-3/4″
  • CUB conveyor brush machines, the EASY T vacuum-table workstation and GCB crossed-belt benches for heavy and shaped parts

GE65 RCC — belt and strip-brush groups

Videos 1 – 2 of 2

Plasma and oxy-fuel cutting leave dross welded to the bottom edge of thick parts, and grinding it off by hand is slow, noisy work. The G65DCC takes it off in one pass: a D belt on a large Ø16-9/16″ soft rubber roller (20 Shore), pressed on by a pneumatic cylinder, works only the edges and breaks the slag away, then 82 strip brushes on two transverse belts, turning in opposite directions, round the edges evenly. Magnetic table and touch screen standard; parts 3/8″ to 4-3/4″ thick, 23-5/8″ wide.

  • D group: abrasive belt on a Ø16-9/16″ (420 mm) 20 Shore roller, pressed by a pneumatic cylinder; pneumatic belt tensioning and photocell belt centering
  • Works the edges, not the face — the slag is broken off without grinding the surface
  • C group: 82 strip brushes on two transverse belts with reverse rotation and independent motors, for an even edge radius
  • Magnetic table and touch screen standard
  • Feed 2.3 – 23 ft/min (0.7 – 7 m/min); suction hoods Ø5-7/8″, 2,354 CFM (4,000 m³/h) required
  • CSA-approved electrical panel; 600 V 3-phase with transformer (208 / 240 / 480 V on request)

Deburring machine buying guide

What we explain to customers before they buy a deburring machine: why deburr and round edges, belts and brushes, which machine for laser, plasma and coated parts, width and small parts, and dust and wet machines. Written by NovoTech's sales engineers and technicians from years of installing machines in Canada.

  1. Why deburr and round the edges?
  2. Belt, brushes or both?
  3. Which machine for which parts?
  4. Width, thickness and small parts
  5. Dust, wet or dry, and running costs

Why deburr and round the edges?

Laser cutting, punching, shearing and plasma cutting all leave something on the edge of a part: a burr, dross or slag, or simply a sharp square edge. A burr cuts hands and gloves, keeps parts from lying flat in the press brake, and spoils welds and fits. On a sharp edge, paint and powder coating pull thin as they cure, so the edge is the first place a coated part rusts. Coating standards for corrosion protection, such as ISO 12944, ask for sharp edges to be rounded to a radius of at least 2 mm (5/64″). A deburring machine does all of this in one pass on a conveyor, where hand grinding takes minutes per part and never comes out the same twice.

Belt, brushes or both?

  • Abrasive belt. A wide belt on a rubber contact roller cuts the burr off and levels the surface. It is the tool for heavy burrs and for a flat, even face.
  • Rotating and oscillating brushes. Top brushes and brush carousels round the edges all around the part, inside holes and slots too, and give the radius a coating needs.
  • Scotch-Brite brushes. Cylindrical non-woven brushes give an even satin grain on stainless and aluminum.

A typical job-shop machine combines a belt, top brushes and a finishing brush, so it deburrs, rounds and finishes in one pass. Parts cut with plasma or oxy-fuel need a machine that breaks the slag off the bottom edge first.

Width, thickness and small parts

Choose the working width from the widest part you run, plus a margin: 6 – 12″ for small laser and punched parts, about 25″ for most job-shop work, and 43 – 53″ for nested parts or full sheets. Thickness is rarely the limit, since these machines take parts several inches thick, but the minimum part size is. A vacuum table holds small parts of any material on the conveyor, and a magnetic conveyor holds small steel parts. Very small or awkward parts are better finished on a brush workstation.

Dust, wet or dry, and running costs

A dry machine needs a dust collector sized for its airflow, from about 1,200 CFM on the small machines to 3,500 CFM on a 53″ machine. We quote a matching collector with the machine. Never run aluminum and steel through the same dry collector: mixed aluminum and steel dust is a fire and explosion hazard. For aluminum, or for mixed materials, a wet machine is the safer choice. It sprays water on every tool, takes the dust to a sludge tank and keeps the parts cool, so they do not deform. Running costs are mostly abrasives: belts, brushes and Scotch-Brite rolls. The right grit sequence for your parts makes them last longer, and we set it up when the machine is installed.

The machines run on 600 V three-phase with a CSA-approved panel (208, 240 or 480 V on request). Send us samples or drawings of your parts with their material, thickness and cutting process. We will propose the groups and abrasives, and can arrange test parts.

Questions we get asked about metal deburring machines

What does a deburring machine do?

It removes the burr that laser cutting, punching, shearing and plasma cutting leave on a part's edges, rounds the sharp edges and evens out the surface — in one pass on a conveyor, where hand grinding takes minutes per part and never comes out the same twice. Deburred parts are safe to handle, bend and weld cleanly, and take paint and powder coating evenly right to the edge.

Belt, brushes or both?

The abrasive belt (R) cuts the burr off and levels the surface; top brushes (X, T) and brush carousels (B4, B8) round the edges, inside holes too; cylindrical Scotch-Brite brushes (B) give a satin finish. Most shops combine them — a belt and top brushes deburr and round in one pass, and a Scotch-Brite brush at the end leaves an even grain.

Which machine for plasma and oxy-fuel parts?

The G65DCC: its D belt on a soft rubber roller breaks the slag off the bottom edge of parts 3/8″ to 4-3/4″ thick and strip brushes on two transverse belts round the edges in the same pass. For thinner plasma parts with light dross a G65 with belt and top brushes is usually enough.

Can it round edges on both sides at once?

Yes — the GT06 and GT13 run strip brushes on cross belts above and below the part and round every edge on both sides in one pass, leaving the faces as they were. The G135RB8 works from the top with the deepest radius, 5/64″ (2 mm).

What do the letters R, X, B, b, B4 and T stand for?

They are Gecam's working groups, named in the order the part meets them. R is an abrasive belt on a rubber contact roller — it takes off the burr and levels the surface. X is a double row of rotating, oscillating top brushes that round the edges, inside holes and slots too, and T a single row of them. B is a cylindrical Scotch-Brite brush for the finish and b an external one below the outfeed. B4 is a carousel of four abrasive brushes for heavier edge rounding. A G65 RXB, for example, deburrs, rounds and satin-finishes in one pass.

Which width do I need?

Take the widest part you cut and add a little margin: 5-7/8″ (G15) and 11-13/16″ (G30) for small laser and punched parts, 25-9/16″ (G65, GM65) for most job-shop work, 43-5/16″ (GM110) and 53-1/8″ (G135) when you deburr nested parts or full sheets. Thickness is not the limit — every machine takes parts up to 4-1/8″ or 4-3/4″ — but the groups are: plain deburring needs a belt, edge rounding needs brushes.

How much dust extraction does a deburring machine need?

Each machine has a hood at every working group and needs a dust collector of the airflow in its table: 1,177 CFM (2,000 m³/h) for the G15, G30, GM65 and GM110, 2,354 CFM for the G65 and 3,531 CFM for the G135. We quote a matching collector with the machine. Wet machines need no dust collector — the water takes the dust to the sludge tank.

Can it handle small parts?

Yes, with the right options: a vacuum table holds small parts of any material on the conveyor, and a magnetic track holds small steel parts. The rubberized pressure rollers before and after each group keep parts flat as they pass. Very small or awkward parts are better finished on a brush workstation such as the EASY T.

Dry or wet?

Dry machines with dust extraction suit most steel and stainless work. Choose a wet machine when the heat of dry grinding would deform the parts, or when you want no grinding dust in the shop: the G30 WET and G65/135 WET spray water directly on every tool and collect the sludge in an external tank.

Why round the edges of laser-cut parts?

A laser or punch leaves a sharp, square edge that cuts hands and gloves and on which paint and powder coating pull thin — the edge is the first place a coated part rusts. A rounded edge is safe to handle and takes an even coat. The G135RB8 rounds edges to a 5/64″ (2 mm) radius; the GT06 and GT13 round the edges evenly on both sides at once.

One side or both sides?

The G135RB8 works the top of the part, with the deepest radius and a belt for heavy burrs. The GT06 and GT13 work the edges from above and below in the same pass, so parts are not turned over and fed twice, and they leave the faces untouched — the choice for galvanized, brushed or pre-painted sheet.

Do the brushes mark coated or brushed sheet?

The strip brushes of the GT06 and GT13 work the edges and leave the faces as they were, so galvanized, brushed and pre-painted surfaces are kept. The G135RB8 is also suitable for surface-treated material; set its belt group off when the face must not be touched.

Brush machine or wide-belt deburring machine?

A CUB brush machine deburrs and satin-finishes flat parts with cylindrical brushes — a clean, even finish and light deburring at a much lower price. Heavy burrs, edge rounding to a set radius and high volumes call for a wide-belt machine with an abrasive belt and top brushes, further up this page.

Which parts suit the EASY T?

Parts a conveyor cannot carry or would not finish evenly: thick plates up to 5-7/8″, heavy brackets, shaped and bent parts, and single pieces. The vacuum table holds the part while the operator guides the brush; the second tool on the arm is a quick tilt away.

What is a crossed-belt bench used for?

Long and shaped parts — profiles, frames, panels, parts with flanges — that the operator deburrs, rounds and satin-finishes against the belt, choosing a roller, shoe or brush and its position along the belt for each job. The motorized table carries parts up to 220 lb, and the optional suction-cup manipulator holds shaped parts in any position.

Do you install the machines and train operators?

Yes. Our technicians install and commission the machine anywhere in Canada, set up the groups and abrasives for your parts and train the operators; abrasives, brushes and parts are supplied from Mississauga, ON, Canada, and we support every machine by video call.

What voltage do these machines run on?

Three-phase machines are supplied for 600 V as standard with a CSA-approved panel; 208 V, 240 V and 480 V 3-phase are available on request, and 240 V single-phase on models whose main motor is 2.2 kW (3 HP) or less — a machine with a motor above 2.2 kW must run on three-phase power and cannot be converted to 240 V single-phase. Manual machines have no electrics.
