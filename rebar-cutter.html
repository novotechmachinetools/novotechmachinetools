Questions we get asked about rebar cutters

Hydraulic or mechanical rebar cutter — which one should I buy?

A hydraulic H series cutter is quiet, needs less maintenance and can stop mid-stroke, which suits construction sites and general shops. A mechanical M series cutter cuts faster (0.8 s per cut) and suits a rebar shop cutting all day. For bar above Ø2″ (50 mm) or continuous heavy work choose the double-acting SH 45 or SH 60.

Can I run a rebar cutter on single-phase power?

The portable POWER 24 has a built-in transformer and runs on 110 V or 220 V single-phase. The H, M and SH cutters are three-phase and are supplied for 600 V; 208 V, 240 V or 480 V are available on request.

What does the 45 / 65 / 85 kg/mm² cutting capacity mean?

The three columns are the manufacturer's ratings for rebar of different tensile strengths: 45 kg/mm² (≈ 64,000 psi), 65 kg/mm² (≈ 92,500 psi) and 85 kg/mm² (≈ 120,900 psi). Canadian Grade 400W rebar has a minimum tensile strength of 540 MPa (≈ 55 kg/mm²), so the 65 kg/mm² column is a safe guide for everyday Canadian bar.

Are replacement cutting blades available in Canada?

Yes. NovoTech stocks cutting blades, hydraulic parts and electrical components for GMS cutters in Mississauga, ON. Blades are reversible and can be turned to a fresh edge before replacement.

Is there a machine that both cuts and bends?

Yes — the MAX 40 combined machine cuts up to Ø1-1/8″ (30 mm) and bends up to Ø1-3/8″ (36 mm). You will find it on the rebar benders page.

Capacities are the manufacturer's ratings for one or more bars at once in steel of 45, 65 and 85 kg/mm² tensile strength (≈ 64,000, 92,500 and 120,900 psi). For Canadian Grade 400W rebar (minimum tensile strength 540 MPa ≈ 55 kg/mm²) use the 65 kg/mm² column as a conservative guide. Bar-size equivalents (Canadian metric bars and US bar numbers) are given for the single-bar capacity.
