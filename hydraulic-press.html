Hydraulic shop presses — H-frame, T-slot and straightening presses, 33 to 441 US tons

A hydraulic shop press is the machine every repair shop, truck and equipment dealer and fabricator reaches for several times a day: pressing bearings, bushings and pins, straightening shafts and bar, bending flat bar and short plate, pushing a die for the odd forming job. NovoTech's premium-line shop presses are H-frame presses built in Turkey from 33 to 441 US tons (30 to 400 t), all with a pressure gauge, relief valve and a CSA-approved panel: the DPM series with two-speed joystick control, the DPM-K and DK with a cylinder that slides across the frame so the ram meets the work instead of the work being shifted under the ram, the AT presses with T-slot tables for your own dies, the AP garage-type press brake that bends 1/4″ and 3/8″ plate, and the DK-S 3-axis straightening presses from 220 to 441 US tons (200 to 400 t). Further down the page, for the plant that runs a die under PLC control cycle after cycle, is NovoTech's premium line of production hydraulic presses: HSA and HDD deep-drawing H-frame presses to 3,307 US tons (3,000 t), HCFP column presses, CFS / CFSS / CFDD C-frame presses, SMC and HHRP hot plate presses, HSPS portal straightening presses and the HDT Eco. Parts, installation and service for every press from Mississauga, ON, Canada. NovoTech's value-line presses, built in China, add motorized workshop presses from 28 to 176 US tons (25 to 160 t), the EBP-10T hydraulic bench press, motorized H-frame presses to 551 US tons (500 t), presses with a brake function, sliding-table, C-frame and four-column presses — the HP-F1/PLC with a PLC touch screen — and XM orbital riveting machines.

  • Premium-line shop presses 33 – 441 US tons (30 – 400 t): DPM, mobile-cylinder DPM-K / DK, T-slot AT, AP press brake, DK-S straightening
  • NovoTech premium-line production presses: H-frame, column and C-frame to 3,307 US tons, deep drawing with die cushion, hot plate SMC presses
  • Siemens PLC, HMI touchscreen and light barriers on the production line; two-speed joystick control on the shop presses
  • NovoTech value line: workshop presses 28 – 176 US tons, H-frame presses 33 – 551 US tons, brake, sliding-table, C-frame and four-column presses, orbital riveters

Hydraulic press buying guide

What we explain to customers before they buy a hydraulic press: the tonnage for pressing, straightening and bending, H-frame, C-frame or column presses, working space and controls, and shop or production presses. Written by NovoTech's sales engineers and technicians from years of installing machines in Canada.

  4. Shop press or production press?

What tonnage do I need?

A shop press is sized by its heaviest regular job, with a margin. For most truck, equipment and fabrication shops that is a 66 – 110 US ton (60 – 100 t) press. That is enough for pressing bearings and bushings, straightening shafts to about Ø3″ and bending flat bar. Go to 165 – 220 tons (150 – 200 t) for large bearings, track pins, heavy shafts and forming plate, and to straightening presses with a moving ram for plates, beams and long bar.

Three things push the size up:

  • Press fits. The interference of the part and its size: a seized bearing can take far more than a new one.
  • Straightening. The diameter of the shaft, and how far apart the supports are.
  • Bending. The thickness and width of the plate or bar, and the die opening. For plate, the press brake formula applies: F = S² × 2 × R ÷ (1.4 × V) tonnes per metre of bend with S and V in millimetres and R = 47 kg/mm² for mild steel — about 575 × t² ÷ V US tons per foot in inches.

Leave about 20 – 30 % headroom. A press that works at its limit every day wears its seals and pump sooner, and the gauge reading gets hard to trust.

H-frame, C-frame or column press?

  • H-frame. Two columns, a fixed table and a ram between them. It is the stiffest and takes the largest tables and tonnages, which makes it the standard shop press and the choice for deep drawing and big dies.
  • C-frame. Open on three sides, so small dies and parts go in and out quickly from the front and sides, and it takes less floor space. Its table and tonnage are limited because the C opens slightly under load.
  • Column (four-post). Open on all sides for die access and automation, with the ram guided on four columns.
  • Moving cylinder. On shop presses with a cylinder that travels across the frame, the ram comes to the work, so a long shaft or frame rail is straightened point by point without being moved.

Working space and controls

Check the clear width between the columns, the daylight (the opening from table to ram), the stroke and the table size against your largest part and its tooling, with blocks or V-blocks under it. On a shop press, an adjustable table height (with pins or a winch) saves stacking blocks. A two-speed pump brings the ram down fast and presses slowly. A hand pump, or an inching control, allows fine pressing. A pressure gauge you can read, and a relief valve set to the rated tonnage, are essential. Two-hand controls and a stroke limit switch are needed for die and bending work.

Shop press or production press?

A shop press is operated by hand, with a joystick, a lever or two-hand buttons, for one part at a time: pressing, straightening and bending. A production press runs a die under PLC control, with programmed speeds, pressures, dwell and return, light curtains instead of guards, and quick die change, for thousands of cycles a shift. For deep drawing (cups, pans, housings), a double-action press with a die cushion holds the blank with its own force so it does not wrinkle. Production presses are built around the die: send us the die drawing and the cycle you need, and we quote the press for it.

Bench presses and orbital riveters

A hydraulic bench press with standard bench and louver press tooling is a small sheet-metal shop in itself. It punches round, square and oblong holes, punches and flares or beads holes for stiffness and hoses, cuts louvers, notches corners, bends short flanges and shears light sheet — the small jobs a turret punch or a press brake would be wasted on in an HVAC or sheet-metal shop. An orbital riveter tilts its riveting head a few degrees and rolls it around the rivet end, forming the head gradually with about a tenth of the force a straight punch would need. The part does not deform, the head is smooth and the machine is quiet: the method for hinges, chain links, brake parts, appliances and hardware.

Safety and installation

A hydraulic press stores enormous force in a small part. Never press a part that can shatter or spring out without a guard or a containment screen; hardened bearings and pins can break explosively. Support the part squarely under the ram, never stand in line with the part, and keep the relief valve and the gauge in good order. In Ontario, a new press may need a pre-start health and safety review before it runs. The motorized presses run on 600 V three-phase with a CSA-approved panel (208, 240 or 480 V on request), and the hand-pump presses need no power.

Questions we get asked about hydraulic presses

What tonnage shop press do I need?

Most truck, equipment and fabrication shops run a 66 – 110 US ton press (DPM 1040-60 to DPM 1570-100): enough for pressing bearings and bushings, straightening shafts to Ø3″ and bending flat bar. Go to 165 – 220 US tons for large bearings, track pins, heavy shafts and forming plate; the DK-S straightening presses from 220 to 441 US tons are for plates, beams and bar that need a moving ram.

What do the model numbers mean — DPM 1040-60?

The first number is the clear working width between the columns in millimetres (1040 mm = 40.9″) and the second the press force in metric tons (60 t = 66 US tons). A 1570-100 press is 61.8″ wide at 110 US tons.

Why a mobile (sliding) cylinder?

On the DPM-K and DK the cylinder head travels across the frame on a hand wheel, so the ram comes to the work — a long shaft, a frame rail or a plate stays clamped while you straighten it point by point instead of shifting the part under a fixed ram. The DK series adds a wider 61.8″ – 74.8″ frame.

Can a shop press bend plate?

The AP garage-type press brake is a hydraulic press delivered with a 5-face prism bottom die and hardened top blade for bending 1/4″ (110 US tons) or 3/8″ (165 US tons) mild steel over 40″ or 60″ — the economical way to bend brackets and short plate without a full press brake. The AT press has T-slot tables for bolting your own forming, blanking and coining dies.

Hand pump, foot pedal or two-hand control?

The DPM and DPM-K presses come with two-speed joystick control; a hand pump for fine pressing, a foot pedal and a limit switch that stops the stroke at a set height are options. The AT and AP presses are two-hand controlled with stepless stroke adjustment by limit switch, as die and bending work requires.

Shop press or production press?

A shop press is operated by hand — joystick or two-hand buttons — for one part at a time: pressing, straightening, bending. A production press runs a die under PLC control with programmed speeds, pressures, dwell and return, light barriers instead of guards, automatic lubrication and remote diagnostics, for thousands of cycles a shift. The NovoTech premium line starts at 33 US tons with the CFS C-frame and goes to 3,307 US tons on the H-frame presses.

Single action or double action — what is a die cushion?

A single action press has one ram. A double action (deep drawing) press adds a die cushion in the lower table — a second cylinder that holds the blank holder with its own programmed force — so a flat blank can be drawn into a deep cup, pan or housing without wrinkling. The HDD H-frame and CFDD C-frame presses have the cushion built in; it is an option on the HCFP column press.

H-frame, column or C-frame?

An H-frame (straight-side) press is the stiffest and takes the largest tables and tonnages — the choice for deep drawing and big dies. A column (four-post) press is open on all sides for die access and automation. A C-frame press is open on three sides, so small dies go in and out quickly and the press takes less floor space; its table size is limited because the C deflects, which is why the C-frame range stops at 276 US tons.

Can the press be built to my die?

Yes — every series of the premium line is built to order in the table size, stroke, daylight and tonnage the die needs, from a 33 US ton C-frame to a 3,307 US ton H-frame, with quick die change, die loading arms, ejector, heated platens or a die cushion as required. Send us the die drawing and the cycle you need and we quote the press around it.

Premium-line or value-line press?

The premium-line shop and production presses are built in Turkey with European hydraulics and Siemens controls, for daily production work, straightening lines and custom sizes. The value-line presses are built in China for the repair shop, the maintenance department and lighter production — the same tonnages at a lower price, with built-on or separate power units.
