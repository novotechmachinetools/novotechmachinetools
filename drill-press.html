Drill presses — gear-head, column, floor and bench drill presses

A drill press holds the drill square to the work and feeds it with the force and control a hand drill cannot give — the first machine tool in most fabrication and maintenance shops. NovoTech's value-line drill presses are built in China to our specification: gear-head drill presses from 1″ to 2″ in steel with eight or twelve speeds, power feed and tapping, inverter drill presses that keep full torque at low speed, belt-drive and stepless column drills, variable-speed bench and floor drill presses with a digital speed display, a drilling and tapping press with automatic reverse, and the vises to go with them. Parts and support from Mississauga, ON, Canada.

  • Gear-head drill presses T-25 – T-50: 1″ to 2″ in steel, 8 – 12 speeds, power feed, tapping to M30
  • TX530 inverter drill presses with two-range gearbox; TB column drills, belt drive or stepless by frequency converter
  • WTZ variable-speed bench and floor drill presses, 1/2 – 5/8″; WTZ-16T drilling and tapping with automatic reverse

T-25 – T-40 gear-head drill presses, 1″ to 1-9/16″ in steel

Drill press buying guide

What we explain to customers before they buy a drill press: the drilling capacity you need, gear-head, belt or inverter drives, the spindle speed formula, tapping, and work holding and power. Written by NovoTech's sales engineers and technicians from years of installing machines in Canada.

  1. What drilling capacity do I need?
  2. Gear-head, belt-drive or inverter?
  3. Spindle speed and feed
  4. Tapping on a drill press
  5. Table, work holding and power

What drilling capacity do I need?

A drill press is rated by the largest hole it drills in mild steel in one pass with a twist drill: 1″, 1-1/4″, 1-9/16″ or 2″. Choose a machine one size above your largest everyday hole. The machine then works with torque to spare, holds its speed and lasts longer. As a guide:

  • 1″. General fabrication and maintenance.
  • 1-1/4″ to 1-9/16″. Structural work and tapping.
  • 2″. Heavy plates and flanges.

Large holes drill faster with a pilot hole a little wider than the web (chisel edge) of the big drill, which cuts the feed force sharply. Holes above the drill press's capacity, and holes in thick plate, are faster and safer with an annular cutter (a hollow core drill), either on the drill press with the right adapter or on a magnetic drill.

Gear-head, belt-drive or inverter?

  • Gear-head. A gear-head drill press changes speed with levers on a gearbox and keeps full torque at low speed, where large holes and taps are cut, without belts to slip. It is the machine for steel fabrication.
  • Belt-drive. It is smoother and quieter at high speed for small holes, but changing speed means moving the belts.
  • Inverter. The speed changes steplessly from the panel, and a two-range gearbox keeps the torque at low speed. It gives the convenience of variable speed with the strength of a gearbox.

Look also at the spindle taper (MT2 on bench drills, MT3 or MT4 on gear-head machines), the quill stroke for deep holes, and a power feed, which drills large holes evenly without the operator leaning on the handle.

Spindle speed and feed

The spindle speed comes from the cutting speed of the material:

rpm ≈ 3.82 × cutting speed (ft/min) ÷ drill diameter (in)

Typical cutting speeds for HSS drills are about 80 – 100 ft/min in mild steel, 30 – 50 in stainless and 200 – 300 in aluminum. A 1″ drill in mild steel therefore runs at about 300 – 380 rpm, and a 1/4″ drill at about 1,200 – 1,500 rpm. Cobalt and coated drills run faster. Feed about 0.004″ per revolution on small drills and up to 0.012″ on large ones. Use cutting oil or coolant in steel and stainless. A drill that screams, smokes or makes powder instead of chips is running too fast or feeding too lightly. Stainless in particular must be fed steadily, or it work-hardens.

Tapping on a drill press

Tapping needs a reversing spindle, low speed and good torque. Drill presses with tapping have forward and reverse, often with automatic reverse at a set depth, so the tap backs out by itself. Check the largest tap the machine is rated for in steel, for example M12 on a bench tapping press or M30 on the T-50. Use the correct tap drill for the thread, cutting oil and a sharp tap. Most broken taps come from a worn tap or the wrong drill size.

Table, work holding and power

Check the throat (spindle centre to column), the distance from spindle to table and base, and the table: round, square, tilting or a box table. Never hold work by hand when drilling large holes. A drill that catches spins the part. Clamp it, or hold it in a drill press vise bolted to the table. A coolant system with a pump keeps the drill and the part cool on production work. The bench and floor drill presses and the inverter column drills run on single-phase power. The gear-head drill presses have three-phase two-speed motors, supplied for 600 V with a CSA-approved panel, or for 208, 240 and 480 V on request. Bolt floor machines down on a level floor.

Questions we get asked about drill presses

Gear-head or belt-drive drill press?

A gear-head drill press changes speed with levers on a gearbox and transmits more torque at low speed — the speeds you need for 1″ and larger holes and for tapping — and it does not slip. A belt-drive drill press is quieter and smoother at high speed for small holes but needs the belts moved to change speed. Inverter (V) machines change speed steplessly from the panel and keep their torque at low speed with a two-range gearbox (TX530).

What drilling capacity do I need?

The rated capacity is the largest hole drilled in mild steel in one pass with a twist drill. Choose a machine one size above your largest everyday hole: a 1″ machine for general fabrication, 1-1/4 to 1-9/16″ for structural work and tapping, 2″ for heavy plates and flanges. Larger holes are faster and safer with an annular cutter on a magnetic drill or on the drill press.

Which drill presses tap?

The WTZ-16T taps M12 with automatic reverse; the TX530 range taps 3/4 – 1″ with forward and reverse; the T-50 and T-50E tap M30 in steel; tapping is an option on most T-25, T-26, T-28 and T-30 models. Tapping needs a reversing spindle and low speed — tell us the largest tap and the material.

Single-phase or 3-phase?

The WTZ bench and floor drill presses and the TB…V inverter column drills run on single-phase power; the gear-head drill presses have 3-phase two-speed motors, supplied for 600 V with a CSA-approved panel or for 208 / 240 / 480 V on request.

What voltage do these machines run on?

Three-phase machines are supplied for 600 V as standard with a CSA-approved panel; 208 V, 240 V and 480 V 3-phase are available on request, and 240 V single-phase on models whose main motor is 2.2 kW (3 HP) or less — a machine with a motor above 2.2 kW must run on three-phase power and cannot be converted to 240 V single-phase. Manual machines have no electrics.
