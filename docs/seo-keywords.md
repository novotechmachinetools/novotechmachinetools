# Keyword research — band saw section (Google Trends, Oct 2026)

Rule for every page of this site: before writing a page title, meta description, H1 and intro,
check which spelling and modifiers people actually search for (Google Trends compare, Canada first,
US as a larger sample), then use the top variants naturally in the title, meta, intro and FAQ.
Both spellings of a compound word are used (one-word and two-word), the dominant one first.

## How the pages use it

* Page titles carry no supplier brand at all (Bekamak, GMS, İşitan, Dener … are barely searched in
  Canada): every title is built from the product's own names and the search variants with the most
  traffic — type (manual / semi-automatic / automatic / double column), "metal", "bandsaw(s)" +
  "band saw(s)", feed / miter wording — with "| NovoTech" as the only brand.  Supplier brands live in
  the H1, meta description and body text.
* URL slugs carry no brand either (a page's supplier or content may change and the URL must keep its
  ranking): the category's search phrase with the two-word spelling, e.g. /manual-band-saws,
  /semi-automatic-band-saws, /automatic-band-saws, /double-column-band-saws; the value-line pages take
  the next-best phrase for the same category (/affordable-semi-automatic-band-saws,
  /automatic-feed-band-saws, /twin-column-band-saws).  Two-word "band-saws" in the slug because every
  slug has a modifier, and with a modifier the two-word form is searched more (table above).  The Bekamak and NovoTech-line pages of the same category are given
  different angles so they don't compete for one phrase: Bekamak = miter / NC / industrial, NovoTech
  line = affordable / value-line / automatic feed / twin-column.  Titles ≤ 70 characters, the
  decisive words in the first 60 (NovoTech, Oct 2026).

* Title: primary phrase with the two-word form + the one-word form in brackets, e.g.
  "Metal Cutting Band Saws & Bandsaws in Canada".
* Meta: "band saw(s)", "bandsaw", "metal cutting", "horizontal", "for sale in Canada", the type
  (manual / semi-automatic / automatic / double column) and the brand line.
* Intro: one natural sentence that carries "horizontal metal cutting band saw", "metal bandsaw",
  "band saw for metal" and "band saw machine"; H1 stays short.
* FAQ on the hub: "Band saw or bandsaw?" so AI assistants and Google see both forms as the same.
* Capacity, blade, HP and model numbers in inches first, mm in brackets (Canadian buyers search both).

## Findings — miter wording (saws that swivel to both sides)

Google Trends, Oct 2026. The long-tail phrases ("double miter band saw", "twin miter band saw",
"dual miter band saw") are below Trends' reporting threshold in Canada and the US, so the broad
modifiers were compared instead:

| term (5 years) | Canada | worldwide | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| miter saw | ~75 | — | US spelling edges out "mitre" even in Canada (BC, Ontario, Quebec "miter"; Alberta, Manitoba "mitre") |
| mitre saw | ~70 | — | |
| dual miter saw | — | high | "dual miter" ≈ "double miter"; both dominated by woodworking (dual-bevel) traffic |
| double miter saw | — | high | the usual term in the metal-cutting trade (Bekamak, Cosen, Hyd-Mech, Kalamazoo) |
| twin miter / twin mitre saw | — | ~0 | UK usage only |
| swivel head band saw | — | low but steady | the US term for pivot saws that cut angles (Jet, Baileigh) |

Rule: use **miter** as the primary spelling with a single "(mitre)" mention per page for the Canadian
form, and spread the terms over the models instead of repeating one: **double-miter** on the best
sellers (BS-315GF, BMS DG, BMSO 270DGS), **dual-miter** on the BMSY DG/DGH and BS-460G, **twin-miter**
on the BMSY DG NC, **swivel-head miter** on the BS-460GB, **single-miter** where the head swivels one
way. Body text may still say "miters to both sides".
