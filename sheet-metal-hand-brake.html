Sheet metal hand brakes — straight brakes, folders and bench brakes

A sheet metal hand brake clamps the sheet under a one-piece beam and swings a folding leaf up around the edge by hand — long, straight, clean bends in thin sheet with no punch and die and no marks on painted material. NovoTech sells three kinds in Canada, all on this page: straight hand brakes from a 12″ bench model to 13 ft, in 22 to 12 ga, for flashing, trim and duct panels; foot-clamp folding machines with one-piece blades from 36 to 119″ whose clamping beam closes from a foot pedal so both hands guide the sheet, to 12 ga; and bench brakes and offset brakes for the bench and the job box. The folding machines with segmented blades are with the box and pan brakes, and the BSM slitting brakes and shear and brake machines with the roofing and siding brakes. Each family below has its full specification table and photos. NovoTech's value-line brakes are built in China to our specification, with parts and support from Mississauga, ON, Canada.

  • Straight hand brakes 12″ – 13 ft, 22 to 12 ga mild steel; counterweighted leaf, adjustable clamping and radius
  • Foot-clamp folding machines 36 – 119″, 22 to 12 ga: pedal clamping, both hands on the sheet, one-piece blades
  • Bench brakes 14 – 39″ and offset brakes, 20 – 18 ga

Questions we get asked about sheet metal hand brakes

Hand brake, box and pan brake or magnetic brake — which do I need?

A hand (straight) brake has a one-piece clamping blade: the best choice for long straight bends — flashing, trim, duct panels. A box and pan brake has a blade made of removable fingers, so it folds the four sides of a box or a pan; with all the fingers in it works as a straight brake too. A magnetic brake has no clamping beam at all — an electromagnet holds a loose steel bar on the sheet — so it folds boxes of any depth, closed and narrow shapes and short return flanges, at the cost of a lower capacity. For 14 ga and heavier, or for production, choose a hydraulic or electric folding machine. Each kind has its own page.

What does a foot-clamp folding machine add over a hand brake?

The clamping beam is closed by a foot pedal through a linkage instead of a hand lever, so the operator keeps both hands on the sheet to position it against the back stop — faster for repeat parts and safer for long, floppy sheet. The folding leaf is still swung by hand, with an adjustable angle stop for repeat bends.

How are the capacities rated, and what about stainless and aluminum?

Capacities are given in gauge of mild steel over the full bending length. Stainless steel bends at about two gauges lighter (a 16 ga brake takes 20 ga stainless), aluminum at about two gauges heavier. Bending at less than full length lets a brake take somewhat heavier sheet; folding heavier material than rated bends the beams permanently.

Can a brake fold painted or coated sheet without marking it?

Yes — the sheet is clamped and the leaf swings around the clamp edge without sliding over a die, so pre-painted and stainless sheet stays clean; a strip of protective film keeps a mirror finish perfect.

What is an offset brake?

An offset brake makes the small Z-shaped step (offset) at the edge of a panel in one stroke, for lap joints and door panels. The ZWJ-20 and ZWJ-36 make 1/2″ offsets up to 90° in 20 ga steel and 18 ga aluminum. For slitting brakes, see the roofing and siding brakes.

What voltage do these machines run on?

Three-phase machines are supplied for 600 V as standard with a CSA-approved panel; 208 V, 240 V and 480 V 3-phase are available on request, and 240 V single-phase on models whose main motor is 2.2 kW (3 HP) or less — a machine with a motor above 2.2 kW must run on three-phase power and cannot be converted to 240 V single-phase. Manual machines have no electrics.
