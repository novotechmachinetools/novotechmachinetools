Tube end forming machines — expanding, reducing, beading and flanging

A tube end former (pipe end forming machine) expands or reduces the end of a tube so two tubes slip together, and the production versions bead, flare, flange, calibrate and emboss the end as well — the machines behind exhaust systems, ventilation and chimney tube, shock absorbers, handrail and furniture connectors. Three ranges on this page, small to large. ISITAN's SFF single-station hydraulic end formers expand and reduce tube to Ø3-1/2″ × 14 ga with one tooling set, for the fabrication shop. CSM's EF end formers carry six forming stations on one ram for tube to Ø2″, the REF machines roll grooves and the ER 30-5 forms, chamfers and grooves in one cycle — the end-finishing stations of a bending cell. Wecotech's Swiss tube end forming machines cover series production: the Easy line of compact expanders, reducers, a straightening press and a punching unit; the Universal line of modular production machines — expanders, reducers, axial and servo rotary formers, a multi-station forming centre and an automatic beading machine to Ø39.4″; the Traditional line built for the C and IO tooling that is standard in the exhaust trade; the Weco line of CNC inner / outer formers with programmable tool rotation for tube to Ø23.6″ and a flanging cell for bellows; and the shearing cutters, tube shear and deburring machine that complete an end-forming cell. Siemens controls, Bosch Rexroth hydraulics or servo drives, CSA-approved panels and support from Mississauga, ON, Canada.

  • ISITAN SFF hydraulic end formers to Ø3-1/2″ × 14 ga · CSM EF six-station formers to Ø2″, REF grooving and ER 30-5 end-finishing machines
  • Wecotech compact expanders and reducers from Ø3/8″ to Ø23.6″, 21 – 26 US tons, 2 – 4 s cycles, touchscreen programming
  • Wecotech CNC inner / outer formers to Ø23.6″ with 8 – 24 radial pistons, Tubimatic machines for standard C and IO tools, beading, flanging, shearing and deburring

What does a tube end former do?

A tube end former changes the shape of the end of a tube so it can be joined to another part without welding, or with an easier weld:

  • Expanding opens the end so the next tube slips inside: exhaust slip joints, telescoping handrail, sleeves.
  • Reducing (swaging) closes the end so it fits into the next tube, a muffler or a hose.
  • Beading rolls or presses a raised ring for a clamp, a hose or a seal.
  • Flaring and flanging turn the end outward for a fitting or a bolted joint.
  • Calibrating sizes the end exactly round for an accurate fit.
  • Grooving rolls a groove for O-rings and quick couplings.

Exhaust and muffler, ventilation and chimney, furniture, automotive, HVAC and hydraulic-line manufacturers all use these machines, often right beside the bender or the saw.

Ram, inner/outer or rotary forming?

  • Ram forming. The end is pushed into, or over, a forming tool by a hydraulic or servo ram. It is the simplest way to expand and reduce. Single-station machines such as the ISITAN SFF do one operation per stroke, and multi-station machines such as the CSM EF carry several tools on one ram, so expanding, reducing, beading and flaring run in sequence without a tool change.
  • Inner/outer (IO) forming. A tool inside the tube and closing jaws or radial pistons around it form the end between them, in one stroke. Shapes come out more precise and more complex: beads, flanges, embossing and calibration in any position on round or shaped tube.
  • Rotary forming. Turning rollers form beads, grooves and flares while the tube or the head rotates. It is the method for clean beads and grooves without marking the tube.

How to size the machine

The forming force grows with the tube diameter, the wall thickness and the strength of the material, and with how far the end is expanded or reduced. A machine is rated for a range of tube OD and a maximum wall in mild steel, for example expanding Ø1-3/8″ to Ø3-1/2″ tube in 14 ga on the SFF 90-1. Stainless and thicker walls need more force and are checked case by case. Larger changes in diameter may need two steps to avoid splitting or wrinkling the end. For production, look at the cycle time and at how the tube is loaded. A machine that clamps, forms and releases in a few seconds, with the operator loading the next tube, keeps up with a bender.

Tooling, change-over and drive

Each end shape and each tube size needs its own tooling: an expanding cone and segments, reducing jaws, bead rolls or IO tools. Ask what the tooling for your parts costs and how long a change takes. On good machines it takes a few minutes with quick-change holders, or none at all on multi-station machines. On the drive:

  • Hydraulic machines offer the most force for the price.
  • All-electric (servo) machines use less energy, run quieter, need no oil, and control the force and position directly.

Questions we get asked about tube end formers

Expanding or reducing — what does the end former do?

An expander opens a tube end so the next tube slips inside (exhaust slip joints, telescoping handrail, sleeves); a reducer collapses the end to fit into the next tube. The ISITAN SFF does both in one station with a hydraulic ram and interchangeable forming tools; the Wecotech Tubex and Tubex-U expand with a cone-driven segmented tool, the Tubin and Tubin-U reduce with collet-type top jaws, and the Tubimatic and Weco-line machines do both — and bead, flare and flange — with inner and outer tools.

What do the CSM EF, REF and ER machines do?

The CSM EF carries six forming stations on one hydraulic ram, so expanding, reducing, beading, flaring and flanging run in sequence without a tool change — the end former to put beside a bender for tube to Ø13/16″, Ø1-3/16″ or Ø2″, in a horizontal or vertical layout. The REF 30 and REF 40 roll a groove into the tube end for O-ring and clamp connections; the ER 30-5 forms, chamfers and grooves the end in one cycle on a trolley that moves between cells. With a CSM WB wire bender the EF50 makes complete exhaust hangers.

What is an inner / outer (IO) tube end former?

A machine with two tools that work on the tube end at the same time: an inner mandrel or expansion tool inside the tube and an outer tool — closing jaws or, on the Weco line, 8 to 24 radial pistons — around it. Between them the end is expanded, reduced, beaded, flared, flanged or embossed to a defined shape in one stroke, in any position on round or shaped tube. The Tubimatic IO machines take the commercially available IO-tools; the Tubex IO-S and Wecomatic run Wecotech's own tooling under CNC.

Which Wecotech line do I need?

The Easy line is for a shop that forms a few hundred ends a day beside a bender or saw: compact, foot-switch operated, 21 US tons. The Universal line is the modular production machine with touchscreen programming, 26 US tons and the options that a production cell needs — heat exchangers, remote maintenance, Profisafe, horizontal version. The Traditional line is the choice when you already own C or IO tooling. The Weco line is for complex, multi-step forming of exhaust, ventilation and chimney parts at 10 – 40 s a cycle, programmed on the machine.

What tube sizes and wall thickness can the SFF form?

The SFF 90-1 expands tube from Ø1-3/8″ to Ø3-1/2″ and reduces ends down to Ø5/16″, in mild steel up to 14 ga; the SFF 60-1 covers tube to Ø2-3/8″. Stainless and thicker walls are quoted case by case — send us the tube OD, wall and the finished end size you need.

Hydraulic or Electra?

Wecotech builds the Tubimatic IO, Tubax-Rotary, Tubax-Multi and Tubecut as Electra versions with Bosch Rexroth servo drives instead of a hydraulic power unit: no oil, no heat exchanger, lower energy use and quieter running, with the forming force and position under direct servo control. The hydraulic versions keep the higher force reserve (37 US tons on the IO 160) and the lower price.

How long does a tool change take?

A few minutes on every machine on this page: the SFF holds its forming set in a quick-change holder; the Wecotech machines have quick-change flanges for the basic tool and the mandrel and quick-change interfaces for the outer jaws, and the Tubimatic C 80 TRE carries three tool stations so three forming steps run without a change at all.

Can the end former do an exhaust slip joint?

Yes — that is the most common job: expand one end of the pipe so the next section slides in, or reduce it to slip into a muffler or flex pipe, then bead it for the clamp. The SFF does the expansion and reduction; a Tubimatic IO or Wecomatic adds the bead, flare and calibration in the same stroke.

Where are these machines made and how are they supported?

The SFF is built by ISITAN in Bursa, Turkey; the Wecotech machines by Wecotech AG in Switzerland, a 60-person company whose customers include 3M, ABB, Akrapovič and American Axle. NovoTech sells, installs and supports both in Canada from Mississauga, ON, Canada, with CSA-approved panels, remote maintenance on the Wecotech controls and tooling to your tube size.

What voltage do these machines run on?

Three-phase machines are supplied for 600 V as standard with a CSA-approved panel; 208 V, 240 V and 480 V 3-phase are available on request, and 240 V single-phase on models whose main motor is 2.2 kW (3 HP) or less — a machine with a motor above 2.2 kW must run on three-phase power and cannot be converted to 240 V single-phase. Manual machines have no electrics.
