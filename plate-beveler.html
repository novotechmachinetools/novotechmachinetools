Plate bevelling machines — portable cold bevellers for plate and pipe, 1/8″ to 4-3/4″ thick

A plate bevelling machine prepares plate and pipe edges for welding: it sits on the edge, feeds itself along it and cuts the bevel cold — V, X, K or Y, at the angle and depth the weld procedure calls for — so there is no flame cut to clean, no heat-affected zone, no slag and no hours of grinding. NovoTech sells the bevellers of CEVISA, built in Vitoria, Spain, since 1969 and used by boiler and tank makers, structural fabricators, shipyards, wind-tower and offshore builders in more than 60 countries. On this page, small to large: the CHP 7, CHP 12 and CHP 12 INV standard shearing bevellers for plate, tube and discs from 1/8″ to 1-9/16″ thick; the CHP 12G, CHP 21G and CHP 21G INV with the bevel angle adjustable from 20° to 45°; the CHP 12G REV and CHP 21G REV that turn over to bevel the lower edge without turning the plate; and the CHP 30G, CHP 60G, CHP 60G INV and CHP 100G milling bevellers, which machine bevels up to 3-15/16″ wide at 15° to 70° in any material — Hardox, Armox, duplex and stainless included — on plate up to 4-3/4″. Supplied with a CSA-approved panel, installed and supported from Mississauga, ON, Canada.

  • Shearing bevellers CHP 7 – CHP 21G: plate and tube 1/8″ – 1-15/16″, bevels to 1-1/8″ at up to 8.5 ft/min
  • Adjustable angle (20° – 45°), inverted (INV) and reversible (REV) models for X and K bevels without turning the plate
  • Milling bevellers CHP 30G – CHP 100G: machined bevels to 3-15/16″, 15° – 70°, any material, plate to 4-3/4″

Plate beveller buying guide

What we explain to customers before they buy a plate bevelling machine: why bevel cold, shearing or milling bevellers, how to size the machine by the bevel face, bevelling both sides, pipe and discs, and running costs. Written by NovoTech's sales engineers and technicians from years of installing machines in Canada.

  1. Why bevel cold?
  2. Shearing or milling beveller?
  3. How to size a beveller
  4. Both sides, pipe and discs
  5. Running costs, power and set-up

Why bevel cold?

A full-penetration weld on plate or pipe needs a prepared edge: a V, X, K, Y or J bevel at the angle and depth the weld procedure gives. Bevels of 30°, 37.5° and 45° are common, often with a small flat land (root face) left at the bottom. Cut with a torch, the bevel leaves slag, scale and a heat-affected zone, and ground by hand it takes hours and never comes out even. A plate bevelling machine sits on the edge of the plate, feeds itself along it and cuts the bevel cold. It is clean, even and ready to weld, at several feet a minute, with one operator who does not have to hold or guide it. Boiler and tank makers, structural fabricators, shipyards, and wind-tower and offshore builders use them for exactly that reason.

Shearing or milling beveller?

A shearing beveller takes the bevel off with a resharpenable shear cutter, in a curled chip. It gives the fastest and lowest-cost bevel per foot in mild steel, stainless and aluminum, on plate and on pipe, and the cutters last about 3,300 ft of bevel before resharpening.

A milling beveller machines the bevel with a milling head of indexable inserts. It gives a machined finish in any material, including wear plate, armour plate, duplex and super duplex stainless. It cuts any angle from 15° to 70°, transition and J bevels, and very wide bevels on thick plate.

For mild steel and stainless in everyday weld preparation, the shearing machine is the economical choice. For hard and exotic materials, J bevels or very thick plate, choose the milling machine.

How to size a beveller

Bevellers are rated by the width of the bevel face they cut and by the plate thickness range, not by the plate thickness alone. A steep or deep bevel on thick plate has a wide face and may need a larger machine than the thickness suggests. Start from your largest joint: its plate thickness, bevel angle and root face. In stainless, a shearing machine cuts about 80 – 90 % of its mild-steel bevel in several passes and about two-thirds in one pass, while milling machines keep their full capacity. Send us the joint detail from your weld procedure and we will check it against each machine.

Both sides, pipe and discs

X and K joints need a bevel on both the top and bottom edge. Turning a heavy plate over takes a crane and time, so there are two better ways:

  • An inverted (INV) machine bevels the lower edge from underneath, and run together with its standard twin it cuts both bevels in one pass.
  • A reversible (REV) machine turns over on its frame in under a minute and does both bevels by itself.

The shearing machines also bevel tube and pipe, on the inside or the outside, with the pipe horizontal or vertical, as well as discs and tank heads. Milling bevellers are for plate and bar.

Running costs, power and set-up

Bevellers cost little to run: the shearing cutters are resharpened, and the milling heads use standard inserts. The machines need little maintenance, and a 20 ft plate edge is bevelled in a few minutes. Plan how the plate will be supported at a comfortable height, on a table or trestles with room for the machine to travel the full edge, and how chips will be collected. The bevellers run on 600 V three-phase with a CSA-approved panel (208, 240 or 480 V on request). We quote the cutters, inserts and angle devices with the machine, and train your operators at delivery.

Questions we get asked about plate bevelling machines

What does a plate bevelling machine do?

It cuts the bevel a full-penetration weld needs on the edge of a plate or pipe — V, X, K or Y, at a set angle and depth — and feeds itself along the edge while it cuts. Compared with a cutting torch and a grinder it leaves a clean, even bevel with no heat-affected zone, no slag and no scale to grind off, ready to weld, and it does it at several feet a minute with one operator who does not have to hold or guide it.

Shearing or milling beveller?

A shearing beveller (CHP 7 to CHP 21G) takes the bevel off with a resharpenable shear cutter: the fastest and lowest-cost bevel per foot in mild steel, stainless and aluminum, on plate and tube up to 1-15/16″ and bevels up to 1-1/8″. A milling beveller (CHP 30G, 60G, 100G) machines the bevel with a milling head: a machined finish in any material — Hardox, Armox, duplex, super duplex — at the same capacity, angles from 15° to 70°, transition and J bevels, and bevels up to 3-15/16″ wide on plate to 4-3/4″.

Which size do I need?

Start from the widest bevel face and the thickest plate you prepare. CHP 7: bevels to 3/8″ on 1/8″ – 5/8″ plate and thin tube, also aluminum. CHP 12 / 12G: 11/16″ bevels on 1/4″ to 1-9/16″ (12G 1-3/16″). CHP 21G: 1-1/8″ bevels on 3/8″ – 1-15/16″ plate, for continuous production. CHP 30G: 1-3/8″ machined bevels to 1-15/16″ plate; CHP 60G: 2-3/8″ on plate to 2-3/4″ (3-15/16″ special); CHP 100G: 3-15/16″ on plate to 4-3/4″. In stainless steel the shearing machines take 80 – 90 % of their mild-steel bevel in several runs and about two-thirds in one run; the milling machines keep their full capacity.

What do INV and REV mean?

An INV (inverted) machine works upside down and bevels the lower edge of the plate, so the lower half of an X or K bevel is cut without turning a heavy plate over; running an INV under its standard twin (CHP 12 + 12 INV, 21G + 21G INV, 60G + 60G INV) cuts both bevels in one pass. A REV (reversible) machine turns over on its frame in under a minute and does both bevels by itself — one machine, one operator.

Can it bevel pipe and discs?

Yes, the shearing models bevel tube and pipe as well as plate: from Ø3-15/16″ inside (Ø5-7/8″ on the CHP 21G family), on the inside or the outside, with the pipe horizontal or vertical, and discs and tank heads from Ø13-3/4″. The milling bevellers are for plate, flat bar and square bar.

What does it cost to run?

Little: the shearing cutters last about 3,300 ft (1,000 m) of bevel and are resharpened, Cevisa rates the machines maintenance-free, and a 20 ft plate edge is bevelled in under three minutes on a CHP 12. The milling heads use standard indexable inserts. We quote cutters, inserts and angle devices with the machine.

What voltage do these machines run on?

Three-phase machines are supplied for 600 V as standard with a CSA-approved panel; 208 V, 240 V and 480 V 3-phase are available on request, and 240 V single-phase on models whose main motor is 2.2 kW (3 HP) or less — a machine with a motor above 2.2 kW must run on three-phase power and cannot be converted to 240 V single-phase. Manual machines have no electrics.
