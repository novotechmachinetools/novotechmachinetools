Laser cutting machines for sheet and plate — enclosed, open-type, plate and sheet-and-tube lasers, 1.5 to 80 kW

A fiber laser cutting machine cuts sheet and plate from a DXF file with a focused beam — mild steel to 1-1/2″ and beyond with the high-power sources, stainless and aluminum to 1″ — at speeds no plasma or punch can match, with a clean edge and no tooling. NovoTech sells five kinds in Canada, each on its own page: enclosed lasers with a laser-safe cabin and an exchange table or pallet changer, the production machine from 4 × 4 ft compact models to 8.5 × 41 ft and 80 kW; open-type single-table and semi-closed lasers, the entry and long-plate machines to 85 ft; large-format plate lasers on ground rails or gantries to 10 × 164 ft with bevel heads; sheet-and-tube combination lasers with a rotary chuck beside the table; and 3D laser cells and coil-fed lines. Each kind comes in a premium line made in Turkey and a value line built to our specification, as sections on the same page. Below, the five kinds compared, the laser power guide and every model in one table of working area, power and speed; every machine comes with a CSA-approved panel, chiller, nesting software, a 1-year warranty and installation, training and parts from Mississauga, ON, Canada.

  • Enclosed: exchange table or pallet changer, 4 × 4 ft compact to 8.5 × 41 ft, 1.5 – 80 kW — premium GFL / Smart closed, value ULE, H series, compact
  • Open-type and semi-closed: single tables 5 × 10 to 85 × 10.5 ft, 1.5 – 60 kW; plate lasers on ground rails and gantries to 10 × 164 ft with ±45° bevel
  • Sheet-and-tube lasers to Ø13.8″ tube; five-axis 3D cells and robot cells; coil-fed lines to 9/16″ plate

Fiber laser cutting machine buying guide

What we go through with customers before they buy a fiber laser: the power for your thicknesses, bed size and exchange tables, oxygen, nitrogen or air, open or enclosed machines, the source and head, and what the laser needs on site. Written by NovoTech's sales engineers and technicians from years of installing machines in Canada.

  3. Oxygen, nitrogen or air?

What laser power do I need?

Laser power sets two things: the thickest plate the machine cuts well, and the speed on the thin sheet that makes up most of the work. As a guide for production-quality cuts in mild steel:

Fiber laser power and mild-steel thickness Stainless and aluminum: about 20 – 30 % thinner at the same power
Laser powerMild steelTypical shop
1.5 – 3 kWto about 1/2″Sheet-metal parts, signs, enclosures, a first laser
6 kWto about 3/4″Job shops cutting mostly sheet with some plate
12 kWto about 1″Job shops and structural fabricators
20 kWto about 1-1/4″Plate work and high-volume sheet
30 – 40 kW and up1-1/2″ and beyondHeavy plate, high-speed thick cutting

The spec-sheet maximum is always higher than these figures, but cuts at the maximum are slow and rough. Size the machine by the thickness you cut every week, not the plate you cut once a year. Most Canadian job shops buying today choose 6 or 12 kW: thick enough for structural work, and fast enough on thin sheet to pay for the machine. Extra power mostly buys speed: doubling the power cuts thin sheet with nitrogen or air far faster than it cuts thick plate with oxygen.

Bed size: single table or exchange table?

The 5 × 10 ft bed (3015) is the Canadian standard. It takes 4 × 8 and 5 × 10 sheets. Choose a 6.6 × 13 ft (4020) or 6.6 × 20 ft (6020) bed if you cut 20 ft plate, or want to nest several sheets without reloading. Large plate lasers on ground rails run to 164 ft long.

A single-table machine stops while the operator unloads the parts and loads the next sheet. It is the lowest price and is fine for a few sheets an hour. An exchange table (pallet changer) swaps tables in 15 – 20 seconds, so the laser cuts while the operator works on the other table. On a busy machine that is the difference between about 40 % and 70 % cutting time, and it is the base for a loading tower later. If the laser will run more than one shift, buy the exchange table.

Oxygen, nitrogen or air?

  • Oxygen. Oxygen burns with the steel and cuts mild steel plate with the least power. It leaves an oxide layer on the edge, which must be removed before powder coating or the paint can flake off at the edge.
  • Nitrogen. Nitrogen blows the melt out without burning it, leaving a clean, bright, weld- and paint-ready edge on stainless, aluminum and mild steel. It is used at high pressure and in large volume, so a busy laser needs a bulk liquid tank or a nitrogen generator rather than cylinders.
  • Compressed air. Air at about 16 bar (230 psi) replaces nitrogen on sheet up to about 1/4″ and brings the gas bill close to zero. The air must be clean and dry: a dedicated compressor with a dryer and filters, sized for the machine.

Most shops cut mild steel plate with oxygen, stainless and aluminum with nitrogen, and the thin mild steel that makes up most parts with air.

Open or enclosed machine, and laser safety

A fiber laser works at a wavelength of about 1 micron. The beam is invisible, and even its reflection off a sheet can injure the eye before you know it. An enclosed machine keeps the beam inside a cabin with laser-safe windows, so people can work next to it with no extra protection. The cabin also captures fumes better and keeps the shop cleaner. An open machine costs less and is easy to load from any side, but it needs a guarded zone, light curtains or fences, and laser-safety glasses rated for the 1,064 – 1,080 nm wavelength for anyone near it. For 6 kW and up, and for any shop where people walk past the machine, we recommend the enclosed laser.

Source, cutting head and control

The parts that decide how the laser cuts and how much it costs to keep running are:

  • The source. IPG, nLIGHT, Raycus, MAX or the maker's own.
  • The cutting head. Precitec, Raytools, BOCI or the maker's own. An autofocus head sets the focus for each material from the cutting database and pierces thick plate faster.
  • The CNC. Its cutting database and nesting software.

Ask three questions about each:

  • Who repairs it in Canada?
  • How long is its warranty?
  • What do the consumables cost? Nozzles, protective windows and ceramic rings are changed regularly, so their price and availability matter more over the life of the machine than small differences in the purchase price.

A heavier, stress-relieved frame and a stiffer gantry hold accuracy at high acceleration over the years. That is the main difference between a production laser built for two shifts and a machine for lighter duty.

What the laser needs on site and what it costs to run

A laser needs:

  • a level floor
  • 600 V three-phase power, through the transformer we supply, or 480 V
  • the chiller that comes with the machine
  • assist gas
  • a dust collector
  • compressed air for air cutting

As an example, a 6 kW 5 × 10 laser draws about 30 kW in total and needs roughly 33 × 20 ft with a pallet changer. In a Canadian shop, keep the building heated: the chiller and the optics must never freeze over a cold weekend or a power cut. Use the antifreeze coolant the maker specifies if the shop can drop below freezing, and drain the water out of compressed-air lines in winter. In Ontario, the guarding and dust collection of a new laser may call for a pre-start health and safety review, and NovoTech prepares it with the installation. To compare machines, send us your material mix (thicknesses, materials and hours a week), and we run the cutting chart and the cost per part for each laser.

Questions we get asked about laser cutting machines

Enclosed, open-type, plate or sheet-and-tube — which laser should I buy?

By how many sheets a day and what they are. An enclosed laser with an exchange table or pallet changer keeps cutting while you load the next sheet and keeps fumes and the beam inside — the production machine, and the one most shops buy from 5 × 10 ft up. An open-type single-table laser stops while you load but costs less and takes sheets from any side — the first laser, and the only kind for 20 to 85 ft plate on a single table. A ground-rail or gantry plate laser is for heavy plate and very long beds, with bevel heads and oxy-fuel. A sheet-and-tube laser adds a rotary chuck beside the table for a shop that cuts both and has room for one machine. 3D cells cut formed parts; coil-fed lines cut straight from the coil. Each kind has its own page with the premium and value lines side by side.

Premium line or value line?

The premium Dener lasers are built in Turkey on frames rated for 1.5 G, with Precitec heads, IPG / nLIGHT sources, the longest life at high duty and parts stocked in Canada — for two shifts a day. NovoTech's own value line and the Senfeng value line are built in China with their own or Raycus sources, in a wider range — 4 × 4 ft compact machines, 60 – 80 kW plate gantries, sheet-and-tube combos, coil-fed lines — at a lower price; NovoTech stocks the parts and does the service for all of them. Same cut quality at the same power; the premium machine is faster between parts and lasts longer at high duty.

What laser power do I need?

Power sets the thickness and the speed. As a guide for mild steel: 3 kW cuts 1/2″, 6 kW 3/4″ (and 1/4″ at over 300 in/min), 12 kW 1″, 20 kW 1-1/4″ and 30 – 40 kW 1-1/2″ and beyond; stainless and aluminum run about 20 – 30 % thinner at the same power, with nitrogen or air assist. Most Canadian job shops buying today choose 6 or 12 kW: thick enough for structural work, fast enough to pay for the machine on thin sheet. Which table size?

The model number is the nominal working area in centimetres: 3015 = 10 × 5 ft (3,000 × 1,500 mm) — a standard 4 × 8 or 5 × 10 sheet; 4020 = 13 × 6.6 ft; 6020 = 20 × 6.6 ft for 6 × 20 plate; 6025 and 8025 = 20 and 26 ft × 8.2 ft; 12526 = 41 × 8.5 ft. The 5 × 10 machine is the Canadian standard; buy the 6020 if you cut 20 ft plate or want to nest several sheets without reloading; the 4 × 4 ft Senfeng compact is for small parts in a small shop.

Single table, double table or pallet changer?

A single-table machine (Dener Smart open and semi-closed, NovoTech ULF, Senfeng compact and NP) stops while you unload and load — fine for a few sheets an hour and the lowest price. A double table or pallet changer (Dener GFL and Smart closed, NovoTech ULE, Senfeng H series) swaps tables in 15 to 20 seconds, so the laser cuts while the operator loads: the difference between 40 and 70 % spindle time on a busy machine, and the base for a loading tower later.

What about assist gas and fume extraction?

Oxygen gives the fastest cut and an oxide edge on mild steel; nitrogen a clean, paint-ready edge on all metals; compressed air at 16 bar replaces nitrogen on sheet to about 1/4″ and cuts the gas bill to near zero — the intelligent air-cutting package is an option on both lines. The machines have pneumatic extraction compartments under the table and connect to a dust collector, which we can supply.

Can the laser be automated?

Yes — a loading / unloading tower that feeds the pallet changer, sheet storage with several shelves for lights-out production, part sorting, and bevel heads for weld preparation. The Dener GFL and closed-type Smart, the NovoTech ULE and the Senfeng H series are prepared for the tower; the Senfeng coil-fed lines go further and cut straight from the coil. Ask us for the automation layout.
