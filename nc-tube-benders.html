NC tube benders

An NC tube bender is a hydraulic mandrel bender whose bend angles are stored in a numeric control: the operator feeds and rotates the tube to a stop and the machine bends each angle to the programmed value. Hydraulic mandrel benders with a 10″ touchscreen NC: the SFB NC line in five sizes from Ø1-1/2″ × 11 ga to Ø3-1/2″ × 8 ga with 100 programs of 20 bends, the SFB NC/C with a servo-rotation carrier and hydraulic collet for series production, the compact SFB 32 benders for Ø1-1/4″ tube, and NC aluminum-profile benders for window and door frames. The pipe bender most exhaust, handrail, furniture and fabrication shops in Canada actually need — mandrel bending with programmed angles, without the price of a full CNC.

  • Ø1-1/4″ × 11 ga to Ø3-1/2″ × 8 ga mild steel tube · 190° · CLR to 9.8″
  • 10″ touchscreen NC · 100 programs × 20 bends · encoder angle measurement
  • Hydraulic mandrel, clamping and pressure die · up to 15.1 ft feed

Questions we get asked about NC tube benders

What is the difference between the SFB NC and the SFB NC/C?

On the SFB NC the operator feeds and rotates the tube by hand to the gauge bracket and the NC controls the bend angle. The NC/C adds a carrier with a hydraulic collet and a servo rotation axis: feed to the carrier's adjustable stops, the servo turns the tube to the programmed angle between bends, and the carrier screen shows feed and rotation. For parts with several bends in different planes it is faster and far more repeatable.

NC or CNC?

An NC bender programs the bend angles; the operator handles feed and rotation (servo rotation on the NC/C). A CNC bender drives all three axes with servos from a drawing. If you bend hundreds of the same multi-plane part a shift, see our CNC tube benders; for mixed work, exhaust, handrails and furniture, an NC bender pays back faster.

What about the mandrel — when do I need it?

Thin-wall tube bent to a tight radius (CLR under about 2× the diameter) wrinkles on the inside and flattens on the outside without support. The mandrel supports the tube from inside; every SFB has a hydraulic mandrel and bends with or without it, and a wiper die and ball mandrel are available for the thinnest walls.

What do you need from me to quote tooling?

Six things: material type, tube or profile size, wall thickness, the smallest centreline radius (CLR) you bend, the maximum bend angle and the maximum tube length. Every bender comes with one die set in the size you request; extra sets, ball mandrels and wiper dies are quoted from the same list.

What voltage do the benders run on?

Three-phase machines are supplied for 600 V as standard with a CSA-approved panel; 208 V, 240 V and 480 V 3-phase are available on request, and 240 V single-phase on the BK-42 and other models with a small enough motor.
