Metal belt grinders, disc sanders and bench grinders

Belt grinders are the finishing machines of the fabrication shop: they take the burr off a cut, blend a weld, square an end and put a chamfer on an edge faster than a hand grinder and with a flatter result. NovoTech's value-line grinders are built in China to our specification: pedestal belt grinders with 3″ and 6″ belts up to 5.5 HP, a mobile floor grinder for weld seams, wide-belt grinders with 4″ and 8″ belts, knife and tool grinders, combination machines with a belt, a disc and a wheel on one motor, disc sanders to 20″, bench grinders and wire-wheel machines, edge chamfering machines, end-surface grinders and the dust collectors that keep metal dust out of the shop. Parts and belts from Mississauga, ON, Canada.

  • Pedestal belt grinders 3 × 79″ and 6 × 79″, 4 – 5.5 HP, to 6,693 ft/min; dust collector built in on the HB models
  • Wide-belt grinders 4″ and 8″, mobile floor grinder, knife grinders, combination belt / disc / wheel machines
  • Disc sanders 12″ and 20″, bench grinders 8 – 10″, chamfering machines to 1/4″, metal dust collectors 494 – 650 CFM

Belt grinder buying guide

What we explain to customers before they buy a belt grinder: platen, contact wheel and slack belt, belt width and motor power, speeds and abrasives, disc sanders and chamfering machines, and dust and safety. Written by NovoTech's sales engineers and technicians from years of installing machines in Canada.

  1. What does a belt grinder do in a fabrication shop?
  2. Belt width and motor power
  3. Belt speed and abrasives
  4. Disc sanders, bench grinders and chamfering machines
  5. Dust and safety

What does a belt grinder do in a fabrication shop?

A belt grinder takes the burr off a cut, blends a weld, squares the end of a bar, puts a chamfer on an edge for welding and grinds a radius, faster than a hand grinder and with a flatter, more even result. The belt is long, so it runs cooler and lasts longer than a disc or wheel of the same grit. A grinder works in three ways:

  • On the platen. Flat grinding and squaring, against a flat plate behind the belt.
  • On the contact wheel. Aggressive stock removal, radii and tube notching. A hard wheel cuts fast, and a soft wheel blends and finishes.
  • On the slack belt. Between the wheels, for contours and blending.

Belt width and motor power

  • 2″ belts (2 × 72″, 2 × 79″) are for knives, tools and detail work.
  • 3″ belts are the all-round width for deburring and blending in a fabrication shop.
  • 4″ and 6″ belts grind wider parts flat and last longer.
  • 8″ belts are for flat sanding of wide parts and for radius grinding.

Power is what keeps the belt cutting under pressure. About 3 – 4 HP on a 3″ belt is a good production grinder, and heavy stock removal on wide belts wants 5 HP or more. A grinder that slows down when you lean on it glazes its belts and wastes time. Most shops start with a 3″ or 6″ pedestal grinder and add a disc sander for flat faces and accurate angles.

Belt speed and abrasives

Run steel and stainless at about 5,000 – 6,500 ft/min with zirconia or ceramic belts. Aluminum, heat-sensitive parts and fine finishing need a slower speed, about 2,500 – 4,000 ft/min, which is why a two-speed or variable-speed grinder is worth having. Choose the abrasive for the job:

  • Aluminum oxide. General-purpose belts for light work and mild steel.
  • Zirconia. Tough belts for steel under heavy pressure.
  • Ceramic. They cut cool and last longest on stainless and hard steels.
  • Non-woven. Surface-conditioning belts for a satin finish.

Work through the grits, for example 36 or 40 for stock removal, then 60 – 80, then 120 or finer for the finish. Do not jump straight from coarse to fine. Keep separate belts for stainless and carbon steel, because iron particles from a steel belt cause rust spots on stainless.

Disc sanders, bench grinders and chamfering machines

  • Disc sanders. They give a flat face and an accurate angle against their table: square ends, weld chamfers, flat parts. Combination machines put a belt and a disc on one stand.
  • Bench grinders and wire-wheel machines. For tool sharpening, wire brushing and small parts.
  • Chamfering machines and end grinders. They put a consistent weld bevel or chamfer on plate edges, tube and bar ends, which is faster and more even than a hand grinder for repeat work.

Dust and safety

Grinding dust settles everywhere and does not belong in anyone's lungs. Connect each grinder to a dust collector made for metal. Sparks reach the filter, so never share it with a woodworking machine, and never grind aluminum and steel into the same dry collector: the mixed dust is a fire and explosion hazard. On bench grinders, keep the work rest within 1/8″ of the wheel and the upper guard within 1/4″, as grinder safety standards call for. Use only wheels rated for the grinder's speed, and wear eye and face protection. The grinders run on 600 V three-phase with a CSA-approved panel (208, 240 or 480 V on request), and on 240 V single-phase where the motor is small enough. Tell us what you grind, the size of the parts and the finish you need, and we will recommend the grinder and the belts.

Questions we get asked about metal belt grinders, disc sanders and bench grinders

Which belt width — 2″, 3″, 4″, 6″ or 8″?

2″ belts (2 × 72″, 2 × 79″) are for knives, tools and detail work; 3″ is the all-round width for deburring and blending in the fabrication shop; 4″ and 6″ belts grind wider parts flat and last longer; 8″ belts are for flat sanding of wide parts and for radius grinding. Most shops start with a 3″ or 6″ pedestal grinder and add a disc sander for flat faces.

What belt speed for steel, stainless and aluminum?

Around 5,000 – 6,500 ft/min for steel and stainless with zirconia or ceramic belts; slower — 2,500 – 4,000 ft/min — for aluminum, for heat-sensitive parts and for fine finishing. The two-speed and variable-speed grinders (S-150, SDD75, BGM-50, SP-2) cover both.

Belt grinder or disc sander?

A belt grinder removes metal faster, grinds radii on the contact wheel and runs cooler because the belt is long; a disc sander gives a flat face and an accurate angle against its table — chamfers for welding, squaring ends, deburring flat parts. The combination machines (SP-6, BTM250) put both on one stand.

Do I need a dust collector for metal grinding?

Grinding dust settles everywhere and is a health and housekeeping problem; a collector at the machine keeps it out of the air. Use a collector made for metal — sparks reach the filter — and never connect the same collector to a woodworking machine: wood dust and hot metal sparks are a fire hazard. The DVS14 and DC650 are metal-only collectors; for heavy production the WD-1800 wet collector is on the deburring machines page.

What voltage do these machines run on?

Three-phase machines are supplied for 600 V as standard with a CSA-approved panel; 208 V, 240 V and 480 V 3-phase are available on request, and 240 V single-phase on models whose main motor is 2.2 kW (3 HP) or less — a machine with a motor above 2.2 kW must run on three-phase power and cannot be converted to 240 V single-phase. Manual machines have no electrics.
