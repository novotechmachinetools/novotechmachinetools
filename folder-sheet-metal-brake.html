Sheet metal brakes and folding machines — box and pan, hand, magnetic, hydraulic, roofing and 3-in-1

A sheet metal brake (folder) bends sheet by clamping it under a straight beam and swinging a folding leaf up around the edge — no punch and die, no marks on painted sheet, and long, straight bends in thin material that a press brake cannot hold flat. NovoTech sells six kinds in Canada, each on its own page: box and pan brakes with removable fingers for boxes and pans; straight hand brakes, foot-clamp folding machines and bench brakes for flashing, trim and duct; magnetic brakes that fold boxes of any depth; hydraulic, electric and CNC folding machines for 10 ga over 10 ft and production panels; portable roofing and siding brakes for the job site; and 3-in-1 shear, brake and roll machines for the small shop. Below, the six kinds compared, every one of the 137 models in one table, the questions we get asked and the buying guide. NovoTech's value-line brakes are built in China to our specification, with parts and support from Mississauga, ON, Canada.

  • Box and pan brakes 12 – 157″ to 12 ga and 14 ga boxes 9″ deep; hand brakes and foot-clamp folders to 13 ft; bench brakes
  • Magnetic brakes 2 – 10 ft, 16 ga, boxes of any depth; hydraulic folders 10 ga × 10 ft; TFM / TFMS CNC folders 14 ga × 12 ft
  • Portable roofing and siding brakes 7 – 12.5 ft with slitter; 3-in-1 shear, brake and roll machines 8 – 80″

Sheet metal brake buying guide

What we explain to customers before they buy a sheet metal brake: hand, box and pan, magnetic and hydraulic folders, how capacity is rated in gauges, bend radius, short flanges and box depth, and what to check. Written by NovoTech's sales engineers and technicians from years of installing machines in Canada.

  1. Which type of sheet metal brake do I need?
  2. How brake capacity is rated
  3. Bend radius, short flanges and box depth
  4. Folder or press brake?
  5. What to check before you buy

Which type of sheet metal brake do I need?

Every sheet metal brake clamps the sheet under a straight beam and swings a folding leaf up around the clamp edge. What differs is how the sheet is clamped and how the leaf is driven.

TypeHow it clampsBest forLimits
Hand (straight) brakeOne-piece clamping blade, by hand or footLong straight bends: flashing, trim, duct panelsCannot fold the fourth side of a box
Box and pan brakeBlade made of removable fingersBoxes, pans and trays; a straight brake when all the fingers are inBox depth limited by the clearance under the fingers
Magnetic brakeElectromagnet pulls a loose steel bar onto the sheetBoxes of any depth, closed and narrow shapes, short returnsLower capacity, about 16 ga to 8 ft
Hydraulic or electric folding machinePowered clamping beam and leaf, programmed angles and back gaugeProduction panels in heavier gauges, 10 ga over 10 ftHigher price; needs power and floor space
Portable roofing brakeHand clamp on an aluminum frameFlashing and trim on the job siteLight gauges only, about 24 ga steel
Combination shear, brake and rollSmall brake on a frame with a shear and slip rollSmall shops, schools and hobby workShort lengths, light gauges

Most HVAC and sheet-metal shops start with a box and pan brake. Add a magnetic brake when boxes get deeper or shapes get more complex, and move to a hydraulic folding machine when gauges get heavier or volume grows.

How brake capacity is rated

Capacities are given in mild-steel gauge over the full bending length: a 16 ga × 8 ft brake folds 16 ga mild steel across its whole 8 ft. Stainless steel bends at about two gauges lighter (a 16 ga brake takes 20 ga stainless), and aluminum at about two gauges heavier. A bend shorter than the full length lets the brake take somewhat heavier sheet, but folding heavier material than rated bends the beams for good.

Steel gauges and their thickness:

  • 24 ga — 0.024″
  • 22 ga — 0.030″
  • 20 ga — 0.036″
  • 18 ga — 0.048″
  • 16 ga — 0.060″
  • 14 ga — 0.075″
  • 12 ga — 0.105″
  • 10 ga — 0.135″

Aluminum gauges are thinner than steel gauges of the same number, so give aluminum in inches (for example 0.040″ or 0.063″) when you ask for a brake.

Bend radius, short flanges and box depth

  • Radius. The inside radius of a folded bend is set by the nose of the clamping beam or fingers, not by a die. A sharp nose gives a tight bend, and radius bars or a radiused nose give larger radii for heavier or harder sheet.
  • Short flanges. The folding leaf needs enough flange to push against, so check the minimum flange of the brake against your shortest return. Magnetic brakes, with narrow and slotted clamp bars, reach the shortest returns.
  • Box depth. On a box and pan brake, the sides already folded must fit under the fingers, so finger height sets the maximum depth of the box. A magnetic brake has no clamping beam, so its boxes can be any depth.
  • Hems. A hem (an edge folded flat on itself) is folded past 135° and then flattened. Check that the brake can fold to the angle you need and has a way to close the hem.

Folder or press brake?

A folder bends from one side with the sheet lying flat, so a long thin panel needs no second person to support it, the finish is not marked because nothing slides over a die, and there are no punches and dies to change. A press brake is the better machine for short heavy parts, plate, tight tolerances on small flanges and many different bends in one part. Many sheet-metal shops run both: the folder for panels, duct and trim, the press brake for brackets and heavy parts.

What to check before you buy

  • Stiffness. The beams and leaf of a long brake must stay straight under load. Look at the section of the beams and at the adjustment for crowning along the length.
  • Leaf and clamp. Counterweights on the folding leaf of heavy manual brakes, and clamping that adjusts for the sheet thickness.
  • Fingers. Finger sets in widths that combine to any box size you make.
  • Angle and back gauge. Angle stops for repeat bends; on powered folders, a programmable back gauge with stored angle programs.
  • Safety. On powered machines, a foot control, an emergency stop and light curtains. In Ontario, a pre-start health and safety review may be required before a powered folder runs.
  • Power. Manual brakes need none. Powered folders come for 600 V three-phase with a CSA-approved panel, or 208, 240 and 480 V on request.
  • Space. Room in front of and behind the brake for the longest sheet.

Questions we get asked about sheet metal brakes

Box and pan brake, hand brake or magnetic brake — which do I need?

A hand (straight) brake has a one-piece clamping blade: the best choice for long straight bends — flashing, trim, duct panels. A box and pan brake has a blade made of removable fingers, so it folds the four sides of a box or a pan; with all the fingers in it works as a straight brake too. A magnetic brake has no clamping beam at all — an electromagnet holds a loose steel bar on the sheet — so it folds boxes of any depth, closed and narrow shapes and short return flanges that a box and pan brake cannot reach, at the cost of a lower capacity (16 ga to 8 ft). For 14 ga and heavier, or for production, choose a hydraulic or electric folding machine.

How are the capacities rated, and what about stainless and aluminum?

Brake capacities are given in gauge of mild steel over the full bending length. Stainless steel bends at about two gauges lighter (a 16 ga brake takes 20 ga stainless), aluminum at about two gauges heavier. Bending at less than full length lets a brake take somewhat heavier sheet; folding heavier material than rated bends the beams permanently.

Can a brake fold painted or coated sheet without marking it?

Yes — that is one of the reasons to fold instead of press-brake bending. The sheet is clamped and the leaf swings around the clamp edge without sliding over a die. The ALB roofing brakes have a silicone insert in the bending beam for pre-painted coil and copper; on the other brakes a strip of protective film on the sheet keeps the finish perfect.

When should I buy a hydraulic folding machine instead of a press brake?

When the work is long panels in thin and medium sheet — duct, enclosures, architectural cladding, flashing — up to 10 ga. A folder bends from one side with the sheet lying flat, so a long panel needs no support or second person, there are no punch and die to change, and painted sheet is not marked. A press brake is the better machine for short heavy parts, for thick plate, for many different bends in one part and for tight tolerances on small flanges.

What does a CNC folding machine add?

A CNC folder (TFM, TFMS) positions the back gauge for every flange and folds to a programmed angle, one bend after the other from a program of up to 99 bends — the operator only turns and feeds the sheet. Programs are stored for repeat parts, the control corrects the angle for springback and folds radii in small steps. It pays for itself on production panels — cladding, cassettes, enclosures, duct — where a hand-set folder needs a skilled operator for every part. The TFMS has a segmented upper beam for boxes.

What do I need to bend roofing flashing on site?

A portable roofing brake: the ALB brakes have an aluminum frame on folding legs, set up in 10 – 15 minutes, take 24 ga steel and 0.032″ aluminum over 7.2 – 12.5 ft, bend to 180° and slit the coil with a roller knife. For the shop, a box and pan brake or a magnetic brake does the same work in heavier gauges.
