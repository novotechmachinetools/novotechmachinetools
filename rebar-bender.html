Single-phase benders light enough for a pickup truck: 110 V or 220 V with a built-in transformer, digital control optional.

Lightweight benders that run on a 110 V single-phase outlet, for small construction jobs and site work. The BT 26 bends up to Ø1″ (26 mm) rebar and, like the BT 24x5, is programmed through a PLC and bends stirrups with its special stirrup pin.

  • Maximum rebar diameter: BT 26 and MG 26 Junior Ø1″ (26 mm) · BT 24x5 Ø15/16″ (24 mm) · MG 20B Ø13/16″ (20 mm)
  • Runs on 110 V single-phase (can be set to run on 220 V single-phase as well); built-in transformer
  • PLC control: programmable bending angle, number of bends and bending sequence (BT 24x5, BT 26)
  • 3 push buttons to select preset bending angles; bending angle adjustable
  • VFD (inverter) drive controls motor power and torque, with overload protection
  • Bends left and right; controlled manually or automatically; foot pedal starts the bending cycle
  • Different bend types are possible, including stirrups with the special stirrup pin (BT 24x5, BT 26)
  • Comes with a full set of dies and tooling for a variety of bar diameters and bending radiuses
  • Comes with a stand for easy operation at a comfortable height; light enough to move between sites
  • CSA-approved electrical panel with door safety interlock and emergency stop button; digital control panel optional
  • 1-year warranty and 7-day money-back guarantee

Stationary rebar benders

Shop benders on a planetary reducer drive. Program up to 3 angles, bend left and right, and make stirrups with the standard pins and rollers.

B series stationary rebar benders

B 26 · B 36 · B 45x1 · B 50 · BD digital-control versions

The B series is the workhorse rebar bender for rebar shops, precast plants and steel-joist (OWSJ) fabricators: up to Ø2″ (50 mm) bars, or several smaller bars at once, with programmable angles.

  • Planetary reducer drive — a B 45x1 bends about 1,100 pieces of rebar in an 8-hour shift
  • Program memory for 3 different bending angles; manual or automatic cycle
  • Bends left and right; adjustable bar stop and support rollers
  • Stirrup bending with the machine's standard pins and rollers; spiral stirrup attachment optional
  • BD series: same machines with a digital display control panel (BD 36 / BD 45 / BD 50)
  • 600 V 3-phase with CSA-approved electrical panel and overload protection

Why bend rebar with a machine?

Rebar gives concrete its tensile strength, from house footings and retaining walls to parking garages, bridges and towers, and on a construction schedule time and precision decide the cost. Most bars have to be bent: hooks, stirrups, ties, cranked bars, spirals and J-hooks. Bending them by hand with a bending bar is slow, tiring and never quite the same twice. A rebar bender makes it a repeatable job that one operator does in seconds per bar. The machine brings five things.

More bends per hour

Hand bending is slow and hard physical work. On a bender the operator places the bar and steps on the pedal, and the machine makes the bend. Most benders take several bars at once, and a programmed bender repeats a whole shape. A stirrup schedule that takes a crew all morning by hand is done in an hour. The crew spends the time it saves on tying, placing and the rest of the project.

Fewer injuries

Bending bar by hand puts heavy force on the back, shoulders and wrists. It causes strains, cuts and musculoskeletal injuries over a season. With a machine the motor and gearbox do the work. The operator stands clear of the bending disc and works the machine by pedal, behind guards, with an emergency stop within reach.

Bends that match the drawing

A wrong bend in a structural bar is a structural problem: the bar does not fit the cage, or the cover and the anchorage are not what the engineer designed. On a bender the angle, the leg length and the bend diameter are set once, so the hundredth bar is the same as the first. The cage fits the forms, and the inspector finds what the drawings show.

Lower cost and less scrap

One operator does the work of several. Fewer bars are bent wrong and thrown away. You can bend in your own yard instead of waiting, and paying, for a fabricator. For contractors, precast plants and rebar shops the bender usually pays for itself on the first projects.

One machine for many sizes and shapes

A project uses bars of many sizes and many shapes. A bender takes bar from the smallest to its rated maximum by changing the pins and rollers. Angles, bend directions and attachments adapt it to hooks, stirrups, offsets, spirals and J-hooks.

Types of rebar benders

  • Manual rebar benders — a hand bender or bending table — cost little and need no power, but every bend takes the operator's strength. They are for a few bars a day on a small job.
  • Portable electric benders run on 110 or 220 V single-phase and are light enough for one or two people to carry. They bend bar up to about Ø1″ (25 mm) on the job site, and the programmable ones also bend stirrups. See the MG and BT portable benders.
  • Stationary electric benders turn a bending disc through a heavy motor and gearbox. They bend bar up to Ø2-3/8″ (60 mm), or several smaller bars at once, in rebar shops, precast plants and large sites. See the B series and the long-table BS series.
  • Hydraulic benders have the strength for heavy bar and the versatility to work away from the shop. Portable hydraulic heads bend dowels and starter bars in place on slabs, walls and columns. See the dowel bar benders.
  • Combined bender-cutters put a bending disc and a cutter on one frame, with one power connection and one operator, so the work goes from cutting to bending without changing machines. See the MAX 40.
  • Stirrup, spiral and mesh benders do one job in volume: the SL stirrup benders repeat a stirrup without re-setting, the SX and AXIS spiral benders roll pile and column spirals, and the HB mesh benders bend welded mesh. For tonnes a day there are the automatic stirrup benders and shear lines.

Capacity and size

Start with the largest bar you bend and its grade. A bender's capacity is rated for one bar of a given diameter in a given steel strength, and it drops for stronger bar. The capacity table also gives how many smaller bars the machine bends at once. Small machines for residential and site work bend bar up to about Ø1″ (25 mm). Industrial machines for the rebar shop go to Ø2-3/8″ (60 mm) and more. Choose a machine that bends the bar sizes and shapes you use most with some margin, rather than one that is always at its limit. The nameplate on every GMS machine shows the capacities for each steel strength. The rebar bender and cutter buying guide lists Canadian bar sizes from 10M to 55M and explains which capacity column fits 400W and 500W bar.

Check the size of the machine as well as its capacity. It has to fit the shop or the yard with room to swing a long bar around the disc. On the long-table BS benders the table supports long bars.

Power supply

Benders are powered by electricity, by hydraulics or by hand. The choice depends on the size of your projects, the power available where the bending is done, and how many bars you bend. Look at the supply you have before you choose a size:

  • Single-phase 110 or 220 V — the portable benders plug into a site panel, a generator or a shop outlet. They suit residential and small commercial work.
  • Three-phase 600 V — the industrial benders for rebar shops need three-phase power, 600 V in most Canadian plants (208, 240 or 480 V on request). Make sure your building has it before you choose a stationary machine.

Programmable and adjustable bending angles

On older benders the angle is set with stops on the disc and changed by hand for every bend. On a modern bender a PLC (programmable logic controller) does it. The operator sets the angles on the control panel, and the machine stores up to three angles with the number of bars and the order of the bends. A stirrup or a bar with several bends is then made by stepping on the pedal. In automatic mode the machine changes the angle and moves on to the next bend in the sequence by itself. What this brings:

  • Precision and consistency — every bend is made to the programmed angle, with no human error, so the quality is the same on every bar.
  • Speed — the operator switches between angles with a button instead of moving stops, which saves the time lost re-setting between bends.
  • Automation — no more adjusting the angle after each bend: the pedal runs the whole programmed sequence.
  • Lower labour cost — the machine does the repetitive part, and the operators do the skilled work.
  • Flexibility — new angles for a new bar list are programmed in seconds, so the machine adapts to every project and shape.
  • Fewer errors — a tired operator does not mis-set a stop at the end of the shift, and complex bars with several bends come out the same every time.
  • More output and less waste — faster cycles mean more bars per shift and quicker turnaround, and fewer wrong bends mean less scrap.
  • A fast payback — the extra cost of a PLC bender is paid back in labour and output.

Spiral and stirrup bending attachments

An optional spiral bending attachment turns a stationary bender into a spiral bender for the spiral stirrups in piles and round columns:

  • Versatility — one machine bends straight-leg shapes and spirals.
  • Time — spirals are made on your own bender as they are needed instead of being ordered.
  • Any diameter — the spiral diameter is set to the drawing, for standard and special designs.
  • Better reinforcement — a continuous spiral confines the concrete of a column or pile and adds to its strength and life.
  • More work — a shop that bends spirals can bid on work that others cannot.

An optional square stirrup attachment bends several stirrups at once: the bars are stacked and bent together. Production goes up, the labour per stirrup goes down, and every stirrup in the stack has the same shape. For stirrups in large numbers there are also the SL stirrup benders.

Stack bending

Stack bending means bending several bars together in one cycle. The capacity table of every GMS bender gives how many bars of each size it bends at once. Stack bending brings:

  • Efficiency — several bars in the time of one, which shortens the bending of a bar list.
  • Precision — every bar in the stack is bent to the same angle and the same shape.
  • Lower labour cost — fewer cycles and fewer hours per tonne.
  • Less waste — bars bent together are the same, so there are fewer rejects.
  • Productivity and a competitive edge — more tonnes per shift, faster turnaround and better prices for your customers.

J-hook tooling, pins and rollers

The bend diameter is set by the pin (mandrel) the bar is bent around, and the shape by the rollers and stops. Check that the bender comes with, or can take, pins and rollers for every bar size and bend diameter you use. If you make J-hooks or eye bolts, check for J-hook tooling: a bending die or form that shapes the bar into the tight curve of a J-hook, sized for the bars you use.

Hardened tooling and parts

Bending pins, rollers and plates take the full force of every bend. Hardened tooling and parts bring:

  • Durability — hardened steel resists wear and the machine lasts longer.
  • No deformation — hardened pins and plates keep their shape under heavy bar.
  • No breakage — they are tough enough not to crack in heavy-duty bending.
  • Consistent bends — tooling that keeps its shape keeps the bends accurate.
  • A lower cost over time — they cost more at first and much less over the life of the machine.

Construction sites and rebar shops are hard on machines, so choose a bender built from good materials, by a maker with a record of reliability. Check that spare parts and service are available in Canada. NovoTech stocks the pins, rollers and parts of the GMS benders in Mississauga, ON, Canada.

What to look for inside the machine

A CSA-approved electrical panel

In Canada every machine has to be inspected by a CSA-accredited inspection body and carry its label. It matters for five reasons:

  • Safety standards — the CSA codes set strict safety criteria and reduce the risk of accidents and electrical hazards.
  • Operator protection — the operator works on equipment that has been inspected.
  • No electric shock — the label confirms that the electrical components meet the safety regulations.
  • Peace of mind — for operators and employers alike.
  • Legal compliance — the CSA label is usually a legal requirement, and a machine without it can bring costly penalties.

Known electrical components

Contactors, relays, overload protection and drives from Siemens, Schneider Electric, Eaton and ABB:

  • Reliability — the electrical system works smoothly, day after day.
  • Current technology — efficient, well-made components in the panel.
  • Safety — built to strict standards, with less risk of electrical faults.
  • Long life — made for harsh industrial conditions, with less downtime.
  • Parts everywhere — sold and supported worldwide, so replacement parts are easy to find in Canada.

Motor and gearbox

The motor and gearbox carry the whole load of the bend. GMS rebar benders have a heavy-duty industrial motor coupled directly to a robust planetary gearbox:

  • Durability — industrial motors and gearboxes are made for heavy loads and continuous work.
  • Consistent performance — the speed holds under load, so the bends stay accurate.
  • Productivity — they take demanding workloads all day.
  • Little maintenance — less repair work and downtime.

Motor overload protection

A thermal overload relay, or a variable-frequency inverter drive, detects excessive current or temperature in the motor and cuts the power before an oversized bar or a jam can damage it:

  • No motor damage — the motor is stopped before it overheats or is overloaded.
  • Longer machine life — fewer costly repairs and replacements.
  • Operator safety — no overheated motor failing during the work.
  • Less downtime — problems are stopped before they halt a project.
  • Efficiency — an inverter drive also sets the motor speed and power to the load, saving energy and wear, and its settings can be adjusted to the machine.
  • Compliance — in Canada, motor overload protection is required by the safety regulations and the CSA codes.
  • Cost savings — lower maintenance costs, no equipment damage, and no liability from accidents.

Safety features

Look for guards around the bending area, a foot pedal, and emergency stop buttons within reach of the operator.

Portable or stationary?

Portability decides where you can use the machine.

Single-phase portable benders, for bar up to about Ø1″ (25 mm):

  • Light — small and compact, easy to carry and move around the site.
  • Easy to move — one person or a small crew moves them, and they fit in the back of a pickup or a van.
  • Used where the bar is placed — they bend on site, near the work, with less handling of the bar.

Three-phase industrial benders, for bar up to Ø2-3/8″ (60 mm):

  • Heavy — far too heavy and bulky to move by hand.
  • Moved with a forklift or crane — they are placed once and usually stay in the shop.
  • Made for the shop — for rebar shops and plants that process tonnes of bar a day, not for moving from site to site.

Budget: the cost per tonne, not the price tag

The price matters, but a cheap machine that stops in the middle of a pour costs more than it saved. Look at:

  • Long-term value — a well-built bender costs more at first and saves it back in uptime, maintenance and scrap.
  • The features you need — the capacity, angles and attachments your work calls for, without paying for what it does not.
  • Quality — a reputable maker and solid construction. Low-priced machines often lack the durability for heavy construction work and cost more in repairs and replacement.
  • Operator efficiency — features that make the operator faster pay back in labour and finish projects sooner.
  • Warranty and support — a solid warranty and parts and service in Canada.
  • Resale value — quality benders from known makers keep their value.

Questions we get asked about rebar benders

Which rebar bender do I need for 15M, 20M and 25M bars?

A B 36 or BS 36 bends single bars up to 35M (#11) and two 20M bars at once in normal steel, and is the usual choice for a rebar shop running 10M to 25M. For site work on 10M–20M a portable BT 26 or MG 26 Junior on 110/220 V is enough. For 30M–45M bars choose a B 45x1, B 50 or the BS 50/BS 60.

Can I run a rebar bender on single-phase power?

Yes — the portable MG 20B, MG 26 Junior, BT 24x5 and BT 26 have a built-in transformer and run on 110 V or 220 V single-phase. The stationary B, BS, SL and SX machines are three-phase and are supplied for 600 V; 208 V, 240 V or 480 V are available on request.

Can a standard rebar bender make stirrups?

Yes. The B series and the portable BT benders make stirrups with the standard pins and rollers; an optional spiral stirrup attachment is available for B and BS series only. For continuous stirrup production from coil use the automatic SLS 12 on the automatic lines page.

What is the difference between the B series and the BS series?

The BS series has a longer table and two bending sleeves, so it bends faster and handles longer, heavier bars — up to Ø2-3/8″ (60 mm) on the BS 60. The B series is more compact and covers Ø1″ to Ø2″ (26–50 mm).

How many bends per shift can a stationary bender make?

A B 45x1 bends about 1,100 pieces of rebar in an 8-hour shift with one operator, with the angle programmed and the machine cycling automatically.
