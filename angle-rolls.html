Angle rolls and roll benders — tube, pipe and profile rollers

An angle roll (also called a roll bender, section bender, profile bender, ring roller or tube roller) pushes a bar, angle, pipe, tube or beam between three rolls to bend it into an arc, a ring or a spiral — the machine behind curved handrails, tank stiffener rings, rolled flanges, arched trusses and coiled heating pipe. The NovoTech premium line covers the complete range: the motorized PK 30 and PK 35 for small flat bar, angle and tube, the hydraulic HPK 40 to HPK 120 section and pipe benders with digital readouts, hydraulic guides and optional NC control, the HPK DP heavy-duty beam benders for channel, I-beam and H-beam up to HEB 600, and the TC-42 NC coiling machine for variable-pitch tube coils. Every machine rolls horizontally or vertically, comes with a standard roll set and takes pipe, tube, angle and special profile rolls; capacity charts for each model are on this page. Sold with a CSA-approved panel, parts and support from Mississauga, ON, Canada. NovoTech's value-line ring rollers, built in China, add motorized RBM and HRBM profile benders with Ø13/16 to 2-1/2″ shafts — the H models with a hydraulic top roll — the ETR-50 electric tube roller and the CRB-76 decorative ring roller.

  • PK 30 / PK 35 motorized and HPK 40 – 120 hydraulic angle rolls — pipe to NPS 5, angle to L 4-3/4 × 4-3/4 × 1/2″
  • HPK 160 – 360 DP heavy-duty beam benders — IPN 600, UPN 600, HEB 600, pipe to NPS 16
  • Digital readouts standard; NC control, hydraulic lateral guides and spiral crane optional; capacity chart per model
  • NovoTech value line: RBM / HRBM motorized profile benders Ø13/16 – 2-1/2″ shafts, ETR-50 tube roller, CRB-76 decorative roller

Angle roll buying guide

Written by NovoTech's sales engineers and technicians from years of installing machines in Canada.

How does an angle roll work?

An angle roll, also called a section bender, profile bender, ring roller or tube roller, bends a bar, angle, channel, pipe, tube or beam by passing it between three rolls. Two rolls drive the section and hold it, and the third is moved in to push it into a curve. Each pass takes the section to a tighter radius, until the arc or ring is finished. A curved handrail, a tank stiffener ring, a rolled flange or an arched truss is made the same way. Because the rolls must grip the section to bend it, the ends of the bar stay straight for a short length. Machines whose outer rolls move independently pre-bend the ends and leave a shorter straight end, and for a full ring you can leave extra length and cut it off.

Why the section matters more than the size

The force an angle roll needs depends on the shape and orientation of the section, not only on its size. A flat bar rolled the easy way (flat) takes far less force than the same bar rolled on edge (the hard way). An angle rolled leg-out is different from leg-in, and a channel flange-in is different from flange-out. That is why every capacity chart lists each profile and position separately, with the largest section and the smallest diameter it can be rolled to. Charts are for mild steel. Stainless, aluminum alloys that are hard to bend and high-strength sections usually need the next model up, and a tighter diameter in the same section also needs a bigger machine. For daily production, choose a machine one size above your largest section: the minimum diameter shrinks and the rolls and shafts last longer.

Twist, distortion and roll sets

Asymmetric sections such as angle and channel tend to twist as they bend, and tube tends to flatten. Three things control it:

  • Lateral guide rolls. Manual or hydraulic, they keep angle and channel square to the rolls.
  • Roll sets. A set shaped for the section supports its walls: pipe rolls for each pipe size, and rolls for angle, channel and special profiles.
  • Delrin or nylon rolls. They roll polished stainless and aluminum without marking.

A standard roll set comes with the machine for flat bar, square bar and similar sections. Rolls for your pipe, tube and profiles are made to your section drawing. Order them with the machine, so the first job is not waiting for tooling.

Horizontal or vertical, NC and spirals

Many angle rolls work in both positions:

  • Vertical. It takes less floor space and suits short pieces and rings.
  • Horizontal. It lets long bars, pipe and beams run out over the floor on supports.

Digital readouts show the position of the bending roll, so a radius can be repeated. An NC control stores the roll positions for each pass and repeats the whole bend for series parts. With a spiral attachment, the machine also rolls spiral staircases, stringers and heating coils, and a dedicated coiling machine rolls variable-pitch coils automatically.

Questions we get asked about angle rolls and roll benders

Angle roll, roll bender, section bender, ring roller, tube roller — are these the same machine?

Yes. All describe the three-roll machine on this page: two driven rolls hold the section and the third roll pushes it into a curve, and a ring is finished by running the piece round again. Each profile — flat bar, angle, square bar, pipe, tube, channel, beam — has its own roll set; the capacity chart under each model lists the largest section it rolls and the smallest diameter it rolls it to.

How do I read the capacity chart?

Find your profile in the left column and read the largest section the machine can roll and, after the arrow, the minimum outside diameter it can roll that section to. A second entry gives a smaller section with its tighter minimum diameter. 'Standard rolls' profiles are rolled with the roll set supplied with the machine; 'optional rolls' need the matching roll set. The chart is for mild steel of 37,700 psi yield; stainless, aluminum and high-strength sections need the next model up.

Which angle roll do I need for 2″ pipe or 2 × 2 × 1/4″ angle?

The HPK 50 rolls NPS 2 pipe to a Ø39″ ring and L 2 × 2 × 1/4″ angle leg-out to Ø24″; the HPK 60 does the same with more margin and takes NPS 3 pipe and L 2-3/4 × 2-3/4 × 1/4″. The HPK 40 to HPK 120 and the PK 35 work in both positions.
