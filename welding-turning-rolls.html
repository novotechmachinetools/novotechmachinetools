Welding turning rolls — welding and pipe rotators, 2,205 to 220,462 lb

Welding turning rolls (welding rotators, tank turning rolls) carry a long round part — pipe, a tank shell, a pressure vessel — on two sets of wheels and turn it under a fixed torch at the welding speed: one section has the motor and the control, the other is an idler placed to suit the length of the part. NovoTech has them all on this page, small to large: light turning rolls from 2,205 lb per set for pipe and small vessels; DKG bolt-adjustable and DSK lead-screw adjustable rolls from 4,409 to 220,462 lb, whose wheel spacing is set to the diameter; DZG self-aligning rolls from 11,023 to 220,462 lb, whose wheel pairs swing to the vessel by themselves; GGLJ pinch-type pipe rotators for pipe segments and flanges and hydraulic fit-up rotators that line up two shell sections for the joint; and pipe stands and supports for fit-up and outboard support. Polyurethane wheels; surface speeds of 3.9 – 39.4 in/min set by VFD on the larger rolls. Each family below has its full specification table and photos. Parts, installation and service from Mississauga, ON, Canada.

  • Light DZG and TRS turning rolls, 2,205 – 11,023 lb per set, work from Ø0.8″
  • DKG bolt-adjustable and DSK lead-screw rolls 4,409 – 220,462 lb · DZG self-aligning rolls 11,023 – 220,462 lb, work to Ø216.5″
  • GGLJ pinch-type pipe rotators, hydraulic fit-up rotators and pipe stands to 19,800 lb

GGLJ pinch-type pipe rotators and hydraulic fit-up rotators, built to order

GGLJ pinch-type · hydraulic fit-up

Two rotators for jobs plain turning rolls do not do. The GGLJ pinch-type rotator holds a pipe between a driven lower roll and an upper roll and turns it around and over within an angle, for joining pipe segments and welding flanges, at 3.9 – 39.4 in/min set by VFD, for manual or automatic welding. The hydraulic fit-up rotator lines up two shell sections for the joint: two hydraulic swing-arm lifting rollers raise, lower and shift each section slightly until the seam is aligned, then the drive roller turns both for welding.

  • GGLJ: pinch rolls for pipe, elbows and flanges; 3.9 – 39.4 in/min (6 – 60 m/h), VFD
  • Fit-up: 1 drive, 2 idler and 2 hydraulic lifting rollers on rails; the drive roller travels electrically
  • Roller positions changed to suit the diameter; built to order

Questions we get asked about welding turning rolls

Self-aligning or adjustable turning rolls?

Self-aligning rolls (DZG) swing their wheel pairs to the diameter by themselves, so vessels of different diameters go on without resetting — the choice when diameters change often. Adjustable (conventional) rolls hold their wheels at a set spacing: the DKG moves the wheel blocks to bolt positions, the DSK sets them with a lead screw and hand wheel. They cost less and suit work that stays in one diameter range for a while.

How are turning rolls rated?

By the weight per set — one drive and one idler section — and by the diameter range the wheels carry. Add the weight of the part and any fixtures, and leave a margin for out-of-round shells and off-centre nozzles; very long work can rest on two idler sections beside the drive.

How fast do the rolls turn the work?

Turning rolls are set by the surface speed of the work, which is the travel speed of the weld: the adjustable and self-aligning rolls run 3.9 – 39.4 in/min (6 – 60 m/h) by VFD, for manual welding and submerged-arc heads; the light DZG rolls reach 43 – 75 in/min and the TRS rolls 56 – 143 in/min.

What does a fit-up rotator do?

It brings two shell sections into line before they are welded: two hydraulic swing-arm lifting rollers raise, lower and shift each section slightly until the edges meet evenly, then the drive roller turns both for welding. The rollers move on rails to suit the diameter.

Positioner or turning rolls?

A positioner holds the part on a turntable or in a chuck and turns it about its own axis, tilted to bring the joint to the flat position: the machine for flanges, short pipe spools, hubs and parts up to about the size of the table. Turning rolls carry a long round part — pipe, a tank shell, a pressure vessel — on wheels and turn it under the torch: the machine for long and heavy work. Long pipe in a positioner chuck needs an outboard support stand at the far end.

What voltage do these machines run on?

Three-phase machines are supplied for 600 V as standard with a CSA-approved panel; 208 V, 240 V and 480 V 3-phase are available on request, and 240 V single-phase on models whose main motor is 2.2 kW (3 HP) or less — a machine with a motor above 2.2 kW must run on three-phase power and cannot be converted to 240 V single-phase. Manual machines have no electrics.
