Corner grinders — corner grinding machines for the weld seams of boxes, cabinets, doors and tube frames

A corner grinder (corner grinding machine, also called a corner sander or weld seam sander) grinds the weld on the corner of a welded box, cabinet, door or tray flush with the sheet, or to a neat radius, ready for paint or polishing — work that takes long minutes with a hand grinder and never looks the same twice. NovoTech sells the corner grinders of Gecam, built in Parma, Italy: the hand-fed 131 and 136, where an abrasive belt on an oscillating fork grinds corners up to 5-7/8″ and 11-13/16″ high to a radius of 3/64″ to 3/8″; the 135, whose head travels the full length of the edge by itself on boxes with edges up to 59″; and PLC-controlled machines for series work — an upper-corner grinder, a six-head grinder and a grinder for the welds of 90° tube frames. Supplied with a CSA-approved panel, installed and supported from Mississauga, ON, Canada.

  • 131 · 136: hand-fed, corners to 5-7/8″ and 11-13/16″ high, radius 3/64″ – 3/8″ or sharp
  • 135: automatic head travel along edges up to 31-1/2″ or 59″, one or two heads
  • Special PLC grinders for upper corners, two corners at once and 90° tube frames

Corner grinder buying guide

What we explain to customers before they buy a corner grinder: what it does, hand-fed or automatic machines, radius, box height and edge length, abrasives and finish, and special machines. Written by NovoTech's sales engineers and technicians from years of installing machines in Canada.

  1. What does a corner grinder do?
  2. Hand-fed or automatic?
  3. Radius, box height and edge length
  4. Abrasives and finish
  5. Machines built for your part

What does a corner grinder do?

When a box, cabinet, door, tray or tube frame is welded at the corner, the weld bead stands proud of the sheet and has to be ground off before the part is painted, powder-coated or polished. With a hand grinder that takes long minutes per corner, and the result depends on the operator. Corners get flat spots, burns and an uneven radius. A corner grinder runs an abrasive belt over rollers or a pad shaped for the corner, so the weld is ground flush or to a set radius, and every corner of every part comes out the same.

Hand-fed or automatic?

On a hand-fed corner grinder (the Gecam 131 and 136), the operator moves the corner along the belt on a work table with squaring guides. These machines are quick to set up and suit mixed boxes and doors in small and medium batches. An automatic corner grinder (the 135) clamps the part and runs the grinding head along the whole edge by itself. It suits long edges and repeat work, and a second head doubles the output. For high volumes of one part, PLC machines grind several corners, or a whole tube-frame corner, in one cycle.

Radius, box height and edge length

  • Radius or sharp corner. The oscillating belt fork of the 131 and 136 grinds a radius from 3/64″ to 3/8″ (R1 – R10 mm), or a sharp corner. Match it to the corner your press brake or design gives, so the ground weld blends into the sheet.
  • Corner height. On hand-fed machines it is up to 5-7/8″ (131) and 7-7/8″ or 11-13/16″ (136). Check your tallest box or deepest door.
  • Edge length. On automatic machines, the length of the edge they run along: 31-1/2″ or 59″ on the 135.

Abrasives and finish

Grind the weld off with a coarse belt, then work through finer grits to match the finish of the sheet. On stainless with a brushed finish, finish with a fine or non-woven belt in the direction of the grain, so the corner disappears into the panel. Ceramic and zirconia belts last longest on stainless. Keep separate belts for stainless and carbon steel, so iron particles never reach the stainless. Connect the grinder to a dust collector for metal, and never mix aluminum and steel dust in the same collector.

Machines built for your part

For series production, Gecam builds corner grinders around the part: upper-corner machines with two heads, six-head PLC machines that grind the corners of a box in one cycle, and four-head machines for 90° tube frames. Send us a drawing or photo of the part and its weld, the material and finish, and the number of parts a shift, and we will propose a standard or a special machine. The machines run on 600 V three-phase with a CSA-approved panel (208, 240 or 480 V on request).

Questions we get asked about corner grinders

What does a corner grinder do?

It grinds the weld bead on an outside corner of a welded box, cabinet, door or tray flush with the sheet — or to a radius — so the corner can be painted, powder-coated or polished without visible weld. The belt runs over rollers or a pad shaped for the corner, so the result is the same on every part, unlike hand grinding.

Hand-fed or automatic?

The 131 and 136 are hand-fed: the operator moves the corner along the belt — quick to set up for mixed boxes and doors. The 135 clamps the box and runs the head along the whole edge by itself, which suits long edges and repeat work, and a second head doubles its output. For high volumes of one part, the PLC machines grind two corners or a whole tube-frame corner per cycle.

What radius can it make?

The oscillating fork of the 131 and 136 grinds a radius from 3/64″ to 3/8″ (R1 – R10 mm), or a sharp corner. The 135 grinds the weld flush; its PLUS version adds vertical, horizontal and oscillating head movement for radiusing.

How high a box can it take?

Corner height up to 5-7/8″ on the 131 and 7-7/8″ or 11-13/16″ on the 136 (19-11/16″ on request). The 135 is limited by edge length — 31-1/2″ or 59″ — and the six-head grinder takes boxes up to 5-7/8″ high.

Can you build a machine for my part?

Yes. Gecam designs special corner grinders around the part — the upper-corner, six-head and tube-frame machines on this page are examples. Send us a drawing or photo of the part and the weld, and the number of parts a shift.

What voltage do these machines run on?

Three-phase machines are supplied for 600 V as standard with a CSA-approved panel; 208 V, 240 V and 480 V 3-phase are available on request, and 240 V single-phase on models whose main motor is 2.2 kW (3 HP) or less — a machine with a motor above 2.2 kW must run on three-phase power and cannot be converted to 240 V single-phase. Manual machines have no electrics.
