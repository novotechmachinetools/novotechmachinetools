Mechanical guillotine shears — direct-gear and flywheel, 1/8″ to 3/8″

A mechanical shear drives the cutting beam from a motor instead of hydraulic cylinders — through a gearbox and crank (direct gear) or a flywheel and clutch — so there is no hydraulic power pack to leak or warm up, the cut is the same on the first and the thousandth stroke, and the machine runs 31 to 55 strokes a minute: the shear for sheet and light plate all day. NovoTech sells two kinds in Canada, both on this page: the premium-line SRGM direct-gear guillotines, built in Turkey with European motors, gearboxes and electrics — the light-type SRGM-H for 1/8″ mild steel and the SRGM for 1/4″ from 43″ to 13.8 ft, the SRGM-HFR / FR with a return-to-sender sheet support that brings the cut piece back to the operator, and the SRGM-HCL / CL built into cut-to-length coil lines; and the value-line Q11 flywheel shears for medium and thick steel, 1/8″ to 3/8″ at 45 to 55 strokes a minute. Every model is in the table below with its capacity, length, strokes, motor and weight, then each family with its full specification and photos. Every machine comes with a CSA-approved panel and a 1-year warranty, installed, trained and supported from Mississauga, ON, Canada. For plate from 3/8″ up see the hydraulic shears; for 20 – 14 ga the sheet metal shears.

  • SRGM-H light type, 1/8″ × 3.6 – 10 ft, 35 strokes/min · SRGM standard, 1/4″ × 3.6 – 13.8 ft, 31 strokes/min — premium line, made in Turkey
  • SRGM-HFR / SRGM-FR with return-to-sender: the cut blank comes back to the front, off-cuts drop to the rear; SRGM-HCL / CL for cut-to-length coil lines
  • Value-line Q11 flywheel mechanical shears, 1/8″ – 3/8″ × 49 – 100″, 45 – 55 strokes/min

Questions we get asked about mechanical shears

Mechanical or hydraulic shear — which is better?

For sheet to 1/4″ a direct-gear mechanical shear is the faster and simpler machine: 31 – 35 strokes a minute from a motor and gearbox, no hydraulic power pack, no oil to heat up, no valves or seals to service, and the same cut quality on the first and the thousandth stroke. Hydraulic shears earn their price from 3/8″ plate upwards, where the beam needs tonnage rather than speed — those are the Dener BS / DAS and the NovoTech value-line QC12K / Q11K on their own pages; the shears overview compares all four lines in one table.

What does direct-gear mean?

The motor drives the cutting beam through a gearbox and crank directly — there is no flywheel and clutch between them, as on an older mechanical shear. The beam makes one stroke per pedal press, stops at top dead centre and cannot repeat a stroke on its own; the motor runs only while the beam cycles, which also makes the machine quiet and economical.

How is the capacity rated, and what about stainless and aluminum?

Capacities are the maker's ratings for mild steel over the full blade length: 1/4″ (6 mm) on the SRGM 1100 and 1360, 3/16″ on the 1560, 5/32″ on the 2060 – 3200 and 1/8″ on the 4200; 1/8″ (3 mm) on the SRGM-H range. Stainless steel is harder to shear, so count on about 60 % of the mild-steel thickness — 14 ga stainless on a 1/8″ machine, 11 ga on a 1/4″ machine; aluminum cuts to the full rated thickness and more.

Which length do I need — 4 ft, 5 ft, 8 ft, 10 ft?

The model number is the maximum shearing length in millimetres: 1100 = 43″, 1360 = 53.5″ (a 4 × 8 sheet cut across), 1560 = 61″ (5 ft), 2060 = 81″ (6.8 ft), 2560 = 101″ (8.4 ft, a 4 × 8 sheet lengthwise), 3060 = 10 ft, 3200 = 10.5 ft, 4200 = 13.8 ft. Most sheet-metal shops run a 2560 or 3060 so a full sheet is cut both ways; HVAC and signage shops are well served by the 1360 – 2060.

What is the back gauge, and is it NC?

The back gauge is the stop behind the blade that sets the width of the cut strip. The SRGM, SRGM-FR and SRGM-HFR come with an NC-controlled back gauge with 29.5″ stroke — the operator keys in the dimension on the controller and the gauge moves there; a 39.4″ stroke, the ELGO P40T or FUJI Monitouch touch-screen control and a front-operated manual gauge with ball screw are options. The SRGM-H has a manual back gauge as standard and the NC gauge as an option.

What does return-to-sender do?

On the FR and HFR series a sheet support behind the blade tilts after the cut and carries the cut piece back under the blade to the front of the machine, while off-cuts fall to the rear. One operator cuts blanks and strips without walking round the machine or needing a helper at the back — the biggest time saver on a shear that cuts small parts all day. The standard version is manual; the pneumatic version adds return-to-rear for scrap.

How long do the blades last, and can they be turned?

The blades are high-carbon, high-chrome tool steel: the top blade has two cutting edges and the bottom blade four, so each is turned to a fresh edge before it needs regrinding. The blade gap is set from the front of the machine to the sheet thickness, which keeps the edges sharp and the cut burr-free. Replacement blades are stocked in Mississauga, ON, Canada.

Can it cut at an angle?

Yes — the optional 0 – 180° angular cutting device on the table positions the sheet for mitre and trapezoid cuts against the squaring arm; the standard squaring arm has a T-slot, a stopper and a ruler for square cuts.

Are the flywheel shears safe to run?

The Q11 mechanical shears make one stroke per pedal press — the clutch engages for one revolution and the beam stops at the top — and are supplied with mechanical hold-downs, a finger guard along the blade and the CSA-approved panel; a rear guard with photocell and front sheet supports are options we recommend for every machine. In Ontario a new shear needs a pre-start health and safety review (PSR) under Regulation 851 before it runs.

What voltage do these machines run on?

Three-phase machines are supplied for 600 V as standard with a CSA-approved panel; 208 V, 240 V and 480 V 3-phase are available on request, and 240 V single-phase on models whose main motor is 2.2 kW (3 HP) or less — a machine with a motor above 2.2 kW must run on three-phase power and cannot be converted to 240 V single-phase. Manual machines have no electrics.
