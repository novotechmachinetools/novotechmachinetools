Aluminum & PVC window and door machinery — welding, cleaning, machining, assembly and complete lines

Window and door shops turn PVC (vinyl) and aluminum profiles into frames in a set order: cut, machine the hardware places, weld or crimp the corners, clean the welds, mill the mullion ends, cut the glazing beads and assemble. NovoTech supplies the machines for every step from Plastmak, built in Bursa, Turkey: single-, double- and 4-corner PVC welders and CNC corner cleaners; a 9-axis profile machining centre, 3-axis CNC drilling and milling machines, copy routers and end milling machines; a hydraulic corner crimper, screwing and sash assembly machines, portable machines and conveyors; and complete production groups for 40 to 220 windows a shift. The saws have their own page — upcut, double-head, bead and miter saws. Every machine is supplied with a CSA-approved panel, installed and supported from Mississauga, ON, Canada. The steps of a window shop

A window or door frame goes through the same steps in every shop, and each step has its machine:

  1. Cutting. The profiles are mitred to length on an upcut saw or a double-head saw.
  2. Machining. Lock and handle places, hinge and espagnolette slots and drainage slots are cut on a copy router or a CNC profile machining centre.
  3. Joining. PVC frames are welded on a single-, double- or 4-corner welder. Aluminum frames are joined with corner cleats and crimped.
  4. Cleaning. On PVC, the weld bead is removed from the faces and gasket grooves on a corner cleaner.
  5. End milling. Mullion and transom ends are milled to fit the frame.
  6. Glazing beads. The beads that hold the glass are cut on a bead saw.
  7. Assembly. Reinforcements, hardware and gaskets are fitted, and the glass is installed.

Size the shop by output

A window shop is a flow of machines, and its output is set by the slowest one. Plastmak's production groups are planned so every step keeps pace with the others, with figures for an 8-hour shift:

  • a PVC single-head group of six machines makes 40 – 60 windows
  • a PVC or aluminum double-head group makes 80 – 120
  • a complete PVC workshop laid out as a loop makes 200 – 220

The real number depends on window sizes and types, the profile system and the people on the line. Start with the windows you sell in a week, add the growth you expect, and choose the group, or the single machine, that removes your bottleneck. The groups share machines, so a shop can start with the single-head group and add a double-head saw and welder, a CNC corner cleaner or a 4-corner welder later.

PVC, aluminum or both?

PVC (uPVC, vinyl) frames are welded and their corners cleaned. Aluminum frames are not welded but crimped over corner cleats, and aluminum machining needs cooled spindles, coolant on the saws and heavier end mills. Many machines handle both: the saws with hydro-pneumatic feeds and cooling, the copy routers and CNCs, and the end mills. If you make both kinds of windows, choose those machines once for both materials and add the welder and cleaner for PVC and the crimper for aluminum.

Your profile system decides the set-up

Window machines are set up for the profile system you use:

  • the clamps and supports of the saws and welders
  • the templates of the copy routers
  • the knives of the corner cleaner
  • the cutters of the end mill
  • the welding temperature and times for each profile

Send us the profile system and samples or section drawings of the profiles before you order, and we will set the machines up for them at installation. Changing to a new profile system later means new knives, templates and settings, so ask what that costs.

Air, power, layout and winter

The machines need three-phase power for their installed load, and compressed air at 87 – 116 psi (6 – 8 bar), dry and filtered, in the volume the machines use. Plan the floor as a loop, with room for the full profile bars (about 21 ft) at the saws, so frames move from one step to the next without crossing back. In a Canadian winter, bring PVC profiles into the heated shop a day before they are cut and welded: cold PVC is brittle, chips at the saw and welds poorly. Tell us the windows and doors you make, the profile systems and the output you need. NovoTech proposes the machines and a layout, installs and commissions them, and trains your operators.

Questions we get asked about window and door machinery

What machines does a PVC window shop need?

At the least a saw, a copy router for the hardware and drainage holes, a welder, a corner cleaner, an end milling machine for the mullions and a glazing bead saw — the six machines of the PVC single-head group, about 40 – 60 windows a shift. Double-head saws and welders double that; a 4-corner welder, CNC corner cleaner and 3-axis CNC take a shop past 200 windows a shift.

What is different for aluminum windows?

Aluminum frames are not welded but joined with corner cleats and crimped — on the SÇ 902 — and the profiles are cut on saws with cooling and hydro-check feeds, machined on copy routers or CNCs with cooled 18,000 rpm spindles and end-milled on the heavier SÇ 211S. The aluminum double-head group puts those machines together.

PVC, uPVC or vinyl?

The same material: rigid PVC, called uPVC in Europe and vinyl in North America. Every machine on these pages that is marked PVC works on vinyl window and door profiles.

Can you plan a complete shop?

Yes. Tell us the windows and doors you make, the profile systems and the output you need; we propose the machines, a layout and the air and power supply — as in Plastmak's workshop plan — then install and commission the line and train your operators.

Do you install the machines and train operators?

Yes. Our technicians install and commission every machine anywhere in Canada, set it up for your profiles and train the operators; spare parts and tooling are supplied from Mississauga, ON, Canada, and we support every machine by video call.

What voltage do these machines run on?

Three-phase machines are supplied for 600 V as standard with a CSA-approved panel; 208 V, 240 V and 480 V 3-phase are available on request, and 240 V single-phase on models whose main motor is 2.2 kW (3 HP) or less — a machine with a motor above 2.2 kW must run on three-phase power and cannot be converted to 240 V single-phase. Manual machines have no electrics.
