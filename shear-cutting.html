Metal shears — hydraulic, mechanical and sheet metal shears, 20 ga to 1-9/16″

A metal shear (sheet metal shearing machine, guillotine shear, shear cutter) cuts plate and sheet to size in one stroke between a fixed bottom blade and a top blade on the moving beam — the first machine in every sheet-metal, HVAC, signage and fabrication shop, before the press brake. NovoTech sells three kinds in Canada, each on its own page: hydraulic shears — swing beam and variable-rake guillotine plate shears from 12 ga to 1-9/16″ and 26 ft, in a premium line made in Turkey and a value line built to our specification; mechanical guillotine shears — direct-gear SRGM machines for 1/8″ and 1/4″ at 31 to 35 strokes a minute and flywheel shears to 3/8″, with no hydraulics at all; and sheet metal shears — pneumatic, electric and bench shears and a coil slitter for the sheet-metal shop. Below, the three kinds compared, every model in one table of capacity, length, strokes, motor and weight, and the questions we get asked; every machine comes with a CSA-approved panel, a 1-year warranty and installation, training and parts from Mississauga, ON, Canada.

  • Hydraulic: swing beam 12 ga – 3/4″ × 4 – 20 ft and guillotine 1/4″ – 1-9/16″ × 8 – 26 ft; premium BS / BSY and DAS CNC, value QC12K / Q11K
  • Mechanical: SRGM direct-gear guillotines 1/8″ × 3.6 – 10 ft and 1/4″ × 3.6 – 13.8 ft, 31 – 35 strokes/min; flywheel shears to 3/8″
  • Sheet metal: pneumatic 16 ga × 52″, electric 14 ga – 5/32″ × 49 – 100″, bench shears, 48″ coil slitting and cut-to-length machine

Which kind of shear do I need?

Every shear cuts between a fixed lower blade and an upper blade on a moving beam. They differ in how the beam is driven and guided, and that sets the thickness, the speed and the price.

TypeTypical rangeBest for
Pneumatic, light hydraulic, electric and bench shears20 ga to 1/4″, 26 – 120″Sheet-metal, HVAC and signage shops cutting a few times an hour
Mechanical guillotineSheet to 1/4″, 30 – 55 strokes a minuteShops that shear sheet all day: fast, simple, no hydraulics
Hydraulic swing beam5/32″ to 3/4″ plate, 8 – 20 ftPlate shops: few moving parts, rugged, economical

On a swing beam shear the beam pivots on two bearings and swings through the cut, and the rake angle is fixed by the design. On a guillotine shear the beam runs straight down in guides and the rake angle can be changed, so the same machine cuts thin sheet flat at a small angle and thick plate at a steeper angle with less force.

How are shear capacity and length rated?

Capacities are given for mild steel (about 65,000 psi) over the full blade length. Stainless steel is harder to shear, so count on about 60 % of the mild-steel thickness: 14 ga stainless on a 1/8″ machine and 3/8″ on a 5/8″ machine. Aluminum cuts to the full rated thickness and about half as much again. Leave a margin: a shear that cuts your thickest plate at its limit every day wears its blades and guides sooner.

For length, think in sheets:

  • A 52″ shear cuts a 4 × 8 sheet across.
  • An 8 ft shear cuts a 4 × 8 sheet lengthwise.
  • A 10 ft shear cuts 5 × 10 sheets both ways, which is why most sheet-metal shops buy an 8 or 10 ft machine.
  • Plate shops buy 10 to 20 ft for long plate and wide strips.

A model number such as 3200 is usually the cutting length in millimetres (3,200 mm = 10.5 ft).

Rake angle, blade gap and cut quality

The rake angle is the slope of the upper blade along its length. A small rake angle cuts flatter strips with less bow and twist but needs more force; a larger rake angle needs less force but distorts the drop piece more, especially narrow strips of thin sheet. A guillotine with variable rake lets you choose: a small angle for sheet, a steeper one for plate.

The blade gap (clearance) between the blades must suit the thickness, typically about 5 – 10 % of it. Too large a gap rolls a burr and bends the edge, and too small a gap wears and chips the blades. Swing beam shears have a single-point gap adjustment by handle or motor, and CNC guillotines set the gap themselves from the thickness keyed in.

Shear blades are usually rectangular with four cutting edges. When an edge dulls, the blade is turned to the next edge, and after all four it can be reground. A burr that keeps growing and a cut that needs more force are the signs it is time to turn the blade.

Back gauge, front arms and sheet support

The back gauge sets the width of the strip. A manual gauge with a counter is fine for occasional cuts. An NC gauge, where the operator keys in the size and the gauge moves there, pays for itself in any shop that cuts many sizes, and a ball-screw gauge set by the CNC holds the size best. Gauge depths are typically 24 – 40″. Choose one deeper than the widest strip you cut from behind.

In front of the blade, support arms with a squaring arm and scale let you cut square and to size from the front, and ball transfers make heavy plate easy to move. Behind the blade, thin sheet sags under its own weight, so the strip comes out narrower than the gauge setting. A pneumatic or mechanical sheet support that holds the sheet up during the cut solves this. On thin-sheet shears, a return-to-sender system brings the cut strip back to the operator.

Safety, installation and what to send us

Every powered shear needs finger guards along the blade, a single-stroke control and a guard at the rear: a photocell or light curtain, or a fence. In Ontario, a new shear needs a pre-start health and safety review (PSR) under Regulation 851 before it runs. Shears are supplied for 600 V three-phase with a CSA-approved panel, or 208, 240 and 480 V on request. Heavy plate shears need a level slab, and we send the foundation details with the quote.   • the thickest and longest cut, and the material
  • the narrowest strip you cut, and the accuracy it needs
  • roughly how many cuts you make in a shift

Questions we get asked about metal shears

Hydraulic, mechanical or sheet metal shear — which should I buy?

By the material and the hours. For sheet to 14 ga in a sheet-metal, HVAC or signage shop, the sheet metal shears are the lowest-priced powered machines — pneumatic, electric or bench, 26″ to 100″. For 1/8″ and 1/4″ all day, a mechanical guillotine shear is the faster and simpler machine: direct-gear drive, 31 – 35 strokes a minute, no hydraulics, with return-to-sender and cut-to-length versions. From 1/4″ plate up — 3/8″, 1/2″, to 1-9/16″ over 20 and 26 ft — the hydraulic shears are the machines, swing beam for the everyday plate shear and a variable-rake guillotine for production and thick plate; each comes in a premium and a value line on the same page. Every kind is installed and supported by the same NovoTech technicians.

Premium line or value line?

The premium hydraulic shears (Dener BS / BSY and DAS CNC) and mechanical shears (SRGM) are built in Turkey on the heaviest frames with European hydraulics, motors and controls and parts in Mississauga, ON, Canada — for two shifts a day and the tightest cut. NovoTech's value line — the QC12K / Q11K hydraulic shears with Estun controls and the sheet metal shears — is built in China to our specification at about two-thirds of the price, the right buy for a job shop. Both lines are sections on the hydraulic shear page, so the comparison is side by side.

Mechanical or hydraulic shear — which is better?

For sheet to 1/4″ a mechanical shear is the faster and simpler machine: 30 – 55 strokes a minute from a motor and gearbox or flywheel, no hydraulic power pack, no oil to heat up, no valves or seals to service, and the same cut quality on the first and the thousandth stroke. Hydraulic shears earn their price from 3/8″ plate upwards, where the beam needs tonnage rather than speed — and in the light sizes a small hydraulic shear is the quietest, lowest-priced way to cut 14 ga to 5/32″ a few times an hour.

Swing beam or guillotine?

On a swing beam shear the beam pivots on two bearings and the cylinders swing it through the cut — few parts, a low price and a rake angle fixed by the design, which is fine for plate. A guillotine shear carries the beam straight down on rollers with a variable rake angle, so the same machine cuts thin sheet flat and fast at a small angle and thick plate at a steeper one; it also keeps the blade gap set automatically. For a plate shop cutting mostly 3/8″ and up, a swing beam shear (Dener BS, NovoTech QC12K); for a job shop that cuts everything from 16 ga to 3/4″ and beyond, a guillotine (Dener DAS, NovoTech Q11K).

How is the capacity rated, and what about stainless and aluminum?

Capacities are the makers' ratings for mild steel (about 65,000 psi) over the full blade length. Stainless steel is harder to shear, so count on about 60 % of the mild-steel thickness — 14 ga stainless on a 1/8″ machine, 11 ga on a 1/4″ machine, 3/8″ on a 5/8″ machine; aluminum cuts to the full rated thickness and about 50 % more. Tell us your thickest material and we size the shear with a margin.

Which length do I need?

The model number is the maximum shearing length in millimetres: 1250 – 1360 = 49 – 53.5″ (a 4 × 8 sheet cut across), 1500 – 1560 = 5 ft, 2000 – 2060 = 6.6 – 6.8 ft, 2500 – 2560 = 8.2 – 8.4 ft (a 4 × 8 sheet lengthwise), 3000 – 3200 = 10 – 10.5 ft (5 × 10 sheet), 4000 = 13 ft, 6000 = 20 ft plate. Most sheet-metal shops run an 8 or 10 ft machine so a full sheet is cut both ways; HVAC and signage shops are well served by 52 – 81″; plate shops buy 10 to 20 ft.
