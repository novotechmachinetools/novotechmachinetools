Pipe sanders and pipe polishing machines — automatic, orbital and centreless tube sanders, Ø3/16″ to Ø15-3/4″

A pipe sander (pipe polisher, tube polishing machine) runs abrasive belts or brushes over round or square tube to grind off rust, scale and weld discolouration, give stainless steel a satin or brushed finish and prepare tube for painting or plating. NovoTech has four kinds: the BT 114-S, NovoTech's premium-line sander with two belts and an automatic feeder for straight and bent tube Ø3/8″ to Ø4-1/2″; the Eurofintec 141 and 144 orbital sanders, whose belts orbit the tube so straight and bent round and oval tube is finished all round without turning, Ø3/8″ to Ø5-7/8″; the Eurofintec 128 S, 128 SL and 128 DL centreless sanders for bar and tube Ø3/16″ to Ø5-7/8″ and the 228 for pipe up to Ø15-3/4″; and the 728 tube brushing machine and 143 tube-end deburring machine. Eurofintec machines are built in Parma, Italy, by Gecam. Supplied with a CSA-approved panel and supported from Mississauga, ON, Canada.

Pipe sander buying guide

What we explain to customers before they buy a pipe sander: which machine for which tube, straight or bent, finish and grit, and keeping stainless clean. Written by NovoTech's sales engineers and technicians from years of installing machines in Canada.

  1. Which pipe sander for which tube?
  2. Straight or bent tube?
  3. Finish and grit
  4. Stainless, dust and installation

Which pipe sander for which tube?

Pipe sanders run abrasive belts or brushes over round or square tube to remove rust, scale and weld discolouration, give stainless a brushed satin finish, or prepare tube for paint, plating or mirror polishing. The kind of tube decides the machine:

WorkMachine
Handrail, furniture and fabrication tube, straight and bent, mixed sizesAutomatic belt sander with feeder (BT 114-S), or orbital sanders (141, 144)
Long runs of straight bar and tube, an even finishCentreless sander (128), two heads for two grits in one pass (128 DL)
Large pipe over Ø5-7/8″Large-pipe sander to Ø15-3/4″ (228)
Burrs on cut tube ends, inside and outTube-end brushing machine (143)

Straight or bent tube?

A centreless sander drives straight tube or bar through its heads between a contact wheel and a work support, for the most even finish at the highest speed. It cannot finish a bend. An orbital sander moves its belts around the tube as the tube is pushed through the head, so elbows, frames and bent handrail are finished after bending, and oval tube too. The BT 114-S head and feeder follow bends up to 45°. If you finish both straight and bent tube, decide which makes up most of your work, or plan two machines.

Finish and grit

The finish follows the belt. A coarse grit (40 – 80) removes rust, scale and weld colour. 180 – 240 grit gives the brushed satin finish of architectural stainless handrail. Finer and non-woven belts prepare the tube for mirror polishing. Work through the grits in steps. On a two-head or two-belt machine, fit a cutting belt and a finishing belt so one pass does both. To match an existing finish, send us a sample: the grit and the belt type decide whether new and old tube look the same.

Stainless, dust and installation

Keep the belts, brushes and supports that touch stainless for stainless only. Iron particles from carbon-steel work embed in the surface and show up later as rust spots. Pipe sanders make fine grinding dust, so connect them to a dust collector, and never run aluminum and steel dust into the same dry collector. Roller stands on the infeed and outfeed support long tube. The machines run on 600 V three-phase with a CSA-approved panel (208, 240 or 480 V on request). NovoTech installs them, sets up the belts for your finish and trains your operators. Tell us the tube sizes and shapes, straight or bent, the material and the finish you need.

Questions we get asked about pipe polishing

Which pipe sander do I need?

For handrail, furniture and fabrication work in mixed sizes, straight and bent: the BT 114-S with its automatic feeder (Ø3/8″ – 4-1/2″) or the 141 / 144 orbital sanders (Ø3/8″ – 5-7/8″, round and oval). For long runs of straight bar and tube to an even satin or polished finish: a 128 centreless sander, two heads on the 128 DL for two grits in one pass. For pipe over Ø5-7/8″: the 228, to Ø15-3/4″. Tube ends go on the 143.

What finish does a belt pipe sander give?

The finish follows the belt: a coarse grit removes rust, scale and weld colour; 180 – 240 grit gives the brushed, satin finish of architectural stainless handrail; finer belts and non-woven belts prepare for mirror polishing. Two belts run at once on the BT 114-S, so a cutting belt and a finishing belt can be fitted together.

Does it polish bent tube?

The BT 114-S and the 141 and 144 orbital sanders do: the BT 114-S head and feeder follow bends up to 45°, and on the orbital sanders the belts orbit the tube as it is pushed through the head, so elbows and frames are finished after bending. Centreless machines finish straight bar and tube only.

Round and square tube on the same machine?

The BT 114-S takes round pipe from Ø3/8″ to Ø4-1/2″ and square tube, passed through flat side by side, two faces at a time. The orbital and centreless sanders are for round tube (the orbital sanders oval too); the 143 deburrs the ends of round and square tube.

How are cut tube ends deburred?

On the 143: the tube end is pushed into a prism against a wire brush that turns while its own axis rotates, deburring the inside and the outside of the end in one cycle — ends cut at 90° or 45°, round Ø13/16″ to Ø4-3/4″ and square to 4-3/4″.

What voltage do these machines run on?

Three-phase machines are supplied for 600 V as standard with a CSA-approved panel; 208 V, 240 V and 480 V 3-phase are available on request, and 240 V single-phase on models whose main motor is 2.2 kW (3 HP) or less — a machine with a motor above 2.2 kW must run on three-phase power and cannot be converted to 240 V single-phase. Manual machines have no electrics.
