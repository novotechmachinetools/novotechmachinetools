Turret mill, universal mill or drill-mill?

  • Turret mill. A Bridgeport-type knee mill: a vertical head that tilts and nods, a quill for drilling and boring, and a knee that raises the table. It is the most flexible machine for one-off parts, slots, pockets and repair work, and most of the turret mills on this page also have a horizontal spindle.
  • Universal mill. It adds a horizontal spindle and arbor for side and slab milling and gang cutters, with a head that swivels through 360°. Heavy universal mills take ISO50 tooling and have power feeds and rapid traverse on every axis, for steel parts that need heavy cuts.
  • Drill-mill. It drills and mills on one column without a knee. It is the lower-cost choice when most of the work is drilling with some face and end milling.
  • Keyseaters and slotting machines. They cut internal keyways and slots that a mill cannot reach.

Table, travels and rigidity

Size the mill by your largest part and the cuts you take, not by the table alone. Check:

  • Table size and the X, Y and Z travels. The part plus its vise or fixture must move fully under the cutter.
  • Distance from spindle to table. Enough for the vise, the part and the longest tool.
  • Table load. The weight the table and knee carry without losing accuracy.
  • Rigidity. Weight, the width of the ways, and the column and knee castings. A heavier machine takes deeper cuts without chatter and holds tolerance longer.

Spindle taper, speed and power

The spindle taper decides your tool holders:

  • ISO40 (NT40). The same size as CAT40 and BT40, for general milling.
  • ISO50. For heavier cuts and larger face mills.
  • R8 and Morse tapers. On small mills.

If you already own tooling, choose the taper it fits, or plan adapters. The speed range must reach low speeds for large face mills and boring in steel, and high speeds for small end mills and aluminum. Motor power sets how much metal you can remove: 3 HP is a typical turret mill and 5 – 7.5 HP a heavy universal mill.

Power feed and DRO

A power feed moves the table at a steady rate. You get a better finish and longer tool life, and the operator does not tire on long cuts. On a manual mill it is the first option to add on the X axis, and heavy mills have power feed and rapid traverse on all three. A digital readout (DRO) shows the table position to 0.0002″ and replaces counting dial turns for most work. With bolt-circle and hole-pattern functions, it makes a manual mill much faster and takes out the backlash errors of the dials.

Tooling, accessories and installation

Budget for the tooling with the machine:

  • a good milling vise and a clamping kit
  • collets or holders for your end mills, and a face mill
  • arbor cutters on a universal mill
  • a rotary table or dividing head for round and indexed work

Set the mill on a level, solid floor and level it carefully, because accuracy starts there. The mills run on 600 V three-phase with a CSA-approved panel (208, 240 or 480 V on request).
