What does a bead roller do?

A bead roller (beading machine, rotary machine or swager) turns sheet metal between two shaped rolls and presses a profile into it as it passes. The roll set decides the job:

  • Stiffening beads. They keep large flat panels from drumming and oil-canning: duct sides, cabinet doors, floor and firewall panels, truck boxes.
  • Edge work. Wired edges, flanges, joggles and offsets on lids, buckets, drums and tanks.
  • Round duct. Crimps and beads on round duct and fittings, so one piece slides into the next and the joint is sealed and stiff.
  • Decorative beads. Lines on body panels and architectural work.

HVAC, sheet-metal, auto body and hot-rod shops all use them, from compact motorized rollers to hydraulic production machines.

Capacity and throat depth

Bead rollers are rated in mild-steel gauge. A deep bead in heavy sheet takes far more force than a shallow bead in light sheet, so a machine rated for 18 ga may form a shallow bead in 16 ga but not a deep one. Stainless needs a heavier machine than mild steel of the same gauge, and aluminum a lighter one. The throat depth, the distance from the rolls to the frame, sets how far from the sheet edge a bead can be formed. For beads across the middle of a panel, choose a deep throat (11″ on the IBKS 1.8). For edge work and duct, a shorter, stiffer throat is better.

Motorized or hydraulic?

  • Motorized bead rollers. A foot pedal and a speed control leave both hands free to guide the sheet. They are the machine for duct, panels and repeat parts, and for anyone forming long beads.
  • Hydraulic top rolls. On machines such as the HIBKS, the top roll is pressed down hydraulically, so the pressure stays constant through the whole bead. Deep beads in heavy sheet form in one pass. They are the choice for production and heavier gauges.

Roll sets

Each profile needs its own pair of rolls: round and flat beads in several widths, offset (joggle) rolls, flanging rolls, wiring rolls, crimping rolls and trimming rolls. Machines come with a standard set, for example four sets on the IBKS 4.0 and eight on the IKM 1.2, and further sets can be made for your profile. Check that the shafts and roll fit are standard on the machine you choose, so extra rolls are easy to get, and that the rolls change quickly when the job changes.

Tips for straight, clean beads

  • Mark the bead lines, set the fence or guide, and feed the sheet straight with both hands.
  • Form deep beads in two or three passes, tightening the rolls a little each time, instead of forcing them in one pass.
  • Stop the bead short of the sheet edge, or run it off the edge, depending on the design. A bead stopped close to an edge distorts the panel.
  • Keep the rolls clean and lightly oiled, so they do not mark galvanized or painted sheet.
