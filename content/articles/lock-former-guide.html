What does a lock former make?

A lock former is a roll-forming machine: the edge of a duct panel is fed into a row of roll stations, and each station bends it a little further until it leaves the machine as a finished seam or connector. With the right roll sets, one machine forms:

  • Pittsburgh lock. The standard longitudinal seam of rectangular duct: a pocket on one edge, into which the 90° flange of the mating edge is pushed and the lip closed over it.
  • Button punch snap lock. A seam that snaps together without hammering.
  • Double seam. For duct fittings and end caps.
  • Drive cleats and S cleats. To join duct sections end to end.
  • Flanges, standing seams and Z shapes.

Most HVAC shops start with a Pittsburgh lock former and add an auxiliary roll former for the other connectors.

Which gauge range?

Rectangular duct is made from galvanized steel from 26 ga on small low-pressure duct up to 18 – 16 ga on large or high-pressure duct, as the duct construction standards (SMACNA) set out for each size and pressure class. Choose the lock former for the heaviest duct you make:

  • the PF20G forms 26 to 20 ga, for residential and light commercial duct
  • the PF16G forms 25 to 16 ga, with six roll forms as standard
  • the AX20G auxiliary roll former makes the joints a lock former does not, such as button locks, Acme double seams, cleats and flanges, with roll sets that change in about five minutes

Running a light machine at the top of its range every day wears the rolls quickly.

Material allowance and set-up

Every seam uses sheet. A Pittsburgh pocket takes about 1″, plus the flange on the mating edge, so allow for both when you lay out the blanks, or the duct comes out undersize. Adjustable edge guides set the sheet so the pocket depth is the same along the whole length. Feed the sheet flat and square to the guide, keep the rolls clean of zinc build-up, and form a test piece whenever the gauge changes. A lock former that forms a straight, even pocket is what makes a duct close without fighting the seam.

The machines of a duct shop

A rectangular duct line usually runs:

  1. a shear to cut the blanks
  2. the lock former to form the seams
  3. a brake (box and pan or folding machine) to bend the corners
  4. a seam closer to close the Pittsburgh seam, faster and neater than a hammer
  5. cleat benders and a hand brake for the cleat edges and flanges

Round duct and fittings add a round duct roller and a power beading and crimping machine. Plan the line so the sheet moves in one direction, from the coil or sheet rack to the finished duct.

Safety, power and installation

Roll formers draw the sheet in. Keep hands clear of the infeed, use the guards and the stop switch, and never clear a jam with the rolls turning. The machines are compact, form at 24 – 40 ft/min, and come with single-phase or three-phase motors: three-phase for 600 V with a CSA-approved panel as standard, or 208, 240 and 480 V on request.
