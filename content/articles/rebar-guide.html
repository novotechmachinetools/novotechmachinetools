Canadian bar sizes and grades

Rebar in Canada is made to CSA G30.18 and sized in metric designations. The machine is sized by the largest bar and the strongest grade you bend or cut, so start from the bar:

The usual grade is 400W, which is weldable, with a minimum yield of 400 MPa and a tensile strength of at least 540 MPa. 500W bar is stronger and needs more force to bend and cut.

Reading the capacity table

GMS rates its benders and cutters in three columns by the tensile strength of the bar: 45, 65 and 85 kg/mm² (about 64,000, 92,500 and 120,900 psi). 400W bar has a tensile strength of at least 540 MPa, about 55 kg/mm², so the 65 kg/mm² column is the safe guide for everyday Canadian bar. For 500W bar, at about 64 kg/mm² and more, the 65 column is at its limit, so use the 85 column for margin. Each table also gives how many smaller bars the machine bends or cuts at once. A B 36 or BS 36, for example, bends single bars up to 35M and two 20M bars together. Bending or cutting several bars at a time multiplies the output, so check the multi-bar figures for the sizes you run most.

Bend diameters, pins and stirrups

The bend diameter is set by the pin (mandrel) the bar is bent around, not by the machine. CSA A23.1 sets minimum bend diameters for hooks, stirrups and ties by bar size and grade, and your drawings and bar lists give the diameters the engineer wants. Check that the bender comes with, or can take, pins for every bend diameter you make, and rollers for every bar size. Standard benders make stirrups with the standard pins and rollers. A spiral attachment rolls spirals and large-radius bars, and for stirrups in quantity an automatic stirrup bender works straight from coil.

Output: bends and cuts per shift

A stationary bender with programmed angles cycles by itself while the operator handles the bar. A B 45x1, for example, makes about 1,100 bends in an 8-hour shift with one operator. A mechanical cutter cuts in under a second per stroke for a shop cutting all day. A hydraulic cutter is quieter, stops mid-stroke and needs less maintenance, which suits sites and general shops. When a shop cuts and bends tonnes a day, an automatic shear line that cuts whole bundles to programmed lengths, and a double bender that bends both ends of a long bar in one cycle, replace a crew. Count your tonnes per day and the number of shapes and lengths, and we will compare the cost per tonne of each option.

Power, site work and winter

The portable benders and the portable cutter have a built-in transformer and run on 110 or 220 V single-phase, so they work on site from a generator or a site panel. The stationary benders and cutters are three-phase, supplied for 600 V with a CSA-approved panel (208, 240 or 480 V on request). On cold Canadian sites, let hydraulic machines warm up before full-capacity cuts, and ask us about a low-temperature hydraulic oil if a machine works outdoors through the winter.
