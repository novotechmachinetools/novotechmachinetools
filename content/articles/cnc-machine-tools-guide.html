CNC lathe, machining centre or boring mill?

  • CNC lathe. It turns round parts: shafts, bushings, flanges, fittings and threads. A slant-bed lathe with gang tooling holds the tools on a slide close to the part, which makes short tool changes and fast cycles on small and medium parts. A turret lathe carries more tools for complex parts.
  • Vertical machining centre (VMC). It mills, drills, taps and bores prismatic parts (plates, brackets, housings, moulds) with an automatic tool changer, usually from one side per set-up. It is the first CNC most job shops buy.
  • Horizontal machining centre and CNC boring mill. A horizontal spindle and a rotary table (B axis) reach four sides of a part in one set-up, and chips fall away from the cut. They are the machines for large housings, frames and heavy prismatic parts, with BT50 spindles for big cutters.

Size: travels, table and swing

On a machining centre, check the X, Y and Z travels against your largest part with its vise or fixture, the table size and the load it carries, and the distance from spindle nose to table for your longest tool. On a lathe, check the swing over the bed and over the cross slide, the turning length, the chuck size and the spindle bore, which sets the largest bar that feeds through. Buy for the parts you make most often, with some margin. A machine that is much too big is slower and costs more to run.

Spindle: taper, speed and power

BT40 spindles (the same size as CAT40) are the standard for vertical machining centres, with fast tool changes and a wide choice of holders. Speeds to about 8,000 rpm suit steel and general work, and higher speeds help aluminum and small cutters. BT50 spindles carry larger face mills and boring bars and take heavier cuts, which is why boring mills have them. Look at the power and torque at low speed if you cut steel with large tools, not only at the maximum horsepower. Through-spindle coolant helps deep drilling.

Control and programming

The control is the part your people use every day. Mitsubishi, Fanuc and Siemens are all supported in North America, with programmers, training and spare parts available locally. Choose the control your programmers already know, or the one your CAM software has a proven post-processor for. Short programs are written at the machine, while most parts are programmed in CAM software on a PC and sent to the machine. We help with the first programs and the post-processor for your CAM system.

Tool changer and options

  • Tool changer. 16 tools cover most job-shop parts, and 24 or more save tool changes on complex work and between jobs.
  • Fourth axis. A rotary table for indexed and round work on a VMC.
  • Probing. A tool setter and a part probe, which cut set-up time and scrap.
  • Chip conveyor and coolant. A conveyor and coolant tank sized for the chips you make.
  • Bar feeder. On a lathe, it lets the machine run unattended on bar work.

Decide on the options when you order. Many are far more expensive to add later.

Foundation, power and temperature

Ask for the foundation drawing and the power requirements before the machine ships. Machining centres and boring mills need a solid, level slab, and heavy machines a foundation of their own. The machines run on 600 V three-phase with a CSA-approved panel (208, 240 or 480 V on request), and need clean, dry compressed air. Accuracy depends on a stable temperature. In a Canadian winter, keep the shop heated around the clock if you can, keep the machine away from doors that open to the cold, and run the warm-up cycle each morning.
