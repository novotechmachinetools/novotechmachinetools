What does a welding positioner do?

A welding positioner holds the part on a turntable and turns it under the torch at a steady, adjustable speed. A round weld on a flange, a pipe-to-plate joint, a hub, a tank head or a wheel is then laid in one continuous pass in the flat position, the easiest position to weld and the one that gives the most even bead. The welder no longer walks around the part or stops to reposition it, and with the torch in a holder the travel speed is as steady as the table. Tilting the table brings the joint to the best angle for the torch. The result is faster welding, fewer starts and stops, and better welds whether the welder is new or experienced.

How much weight can it take?

Positioners are rated for a part centred on the table, flat and tilted. The WP 100 carries 220 lb flat and 110 lb tilted, and the WP 300 661 lb flat and 331 lb tilted. A part whose centre of gravity sits off the centre of the table, or high above it, puts a turning load (torque) on the drive and the tilt, so it needs more capacity than its weight alone. Long parts, overhanging fixtures and chucks all add to it. Leave a margin: choose a positioner rated well above your heaviest part, especially if you weld it tilted.

How fast should the table turn?

The table speed comes from the travel speed the weld needs:

table speed (rpm) = travel speed ÷ (π × weld diameter)

For example, a MIG weld at 20 in/min around a Ø6″ flange needs 20 ÷ (3.14 × 6) ≈ 1.1 rpm. Check that the positioner's speed range covers both your smallest and largest weld diameters at the travel speeds of your processes: TIG is slow, and MIG and flux-core are faster. The WP turntables run from 1 to 5.5 rpm, set on the control box and started from the foot pedal.

Fixtures, chucks and grounding

A T-slotted table takes clamps, angle plates and fixtures, and a 3-jaw lathe chuck holds pipe, shafts and round parts on centre, so the weld runs true. For repeat parts, a custom work plate with the fixture holes saves set-up time. Check how the positioner carries the welding current: a rotary ground on the table gives the current a clean path, so it does not arc through the bearings and gears. Connect the welding ground where the maker specifies.

Power and set-up

The WP positioners run on single-phase 220 V, so no three-phase supply is needed, and they come with a foot pedal. Bolt the positioner to a bench or stand at a comfortable working height, with room around it for the part to tilt.
