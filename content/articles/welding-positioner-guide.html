What does a welding positioner do?

A welding positioner holds the part on a turntable and turns it under the torch at a steady, adjustable speed. A round weld on a flange, a pipe-to-plate joint, a hub, a tank head or a wheel is then laid in one continuous pass in the flat position, the easiest position to weld and the one that gives the most even bead. The welder no longer walks around the part or stops to reposition it, and with the torch in a holder the travel speed is as steady as the table. Tilting the table brings the joint to the best angle for the torch. The result is faster welding, fewer starts and stops, and better welds whether the welder is new or experienced.

Positioner or turning rolls?

A positioner turns the part about its own axis on a table or in a chuck: the machine for flanges, short pipe spools, hubs, wheels and parts up to about the size of the table. Turning rolls (welding rotators) carry a long round part — pipe, a tank shell, a pressure vessel — on two sets of wheels and turn it under a fixed torch: one set has the motor and the control, the other is an idler placed to suit the length of the part. A long pipe held in a positioner chuck needs a support stand at the far end, with rollers that let it turn.

How much weight can it take?

Positioners are rated for a part centred on the table, flat and tilted. The premium-line WP 100 carries 220 lb flat and 110 lb tilted, and the WP 300 661 lb flat and 331 lb tilted; the value-line positioners run from 33 lb on the MRT-15 rotary table to 8,818 lb on the WP-3000, which is rated with the part's centre of gravity up to 12″ from the table centre and 12″ above the table. A part whose centre of gravity sits off the centre of the table, or high above it, puts a turning load (torque) on the drive and the tilt, so it needs more capacity than its weight alone. Long parts, overhanging fixtures and chucks all add to it. Leave a margin: choose a positioner rated well above your heaviest part, especially if you weld it tilted.

How fast should the table turn?

The table speed comes from the travel speed the weld needs:

table speed (rpm) = travel speed ÷ (π × weld diameter)

For example, a MIG weld at 20 in/min around a Ø6″ flange needs 20 ÷ (3.14 × 6) ≈ 1.1 rpm. Check that the positioner's speed range covers both your smallest and largest weld diameters at the travel speeds of your processes: TIG is slow, and MIG and flux-core are faster. The premium-line WP turntables run from 1 to 5.5 rpm, the value-line tilting positioners from 0 to 5 rpm and the heavy ones from 0.1 to 1.2 rpm for large diameters. On turning rolls the speed is set as the surface speed of the work, which is the travel speed of the weld itself.

Fixtures, chucks and grounding

A T-slotted table takes clamps, angle plates and fixtures, and a 3-jaw chuck holds pipe, shafts and round parts on centre, so the weld runs true. A hollow bore lets long tube pass through the chuck, and gas purge through the centre (WP-50) back-purges stainless tube. A torch holder on an adjustable arm keeps the torch in one place while the part turns. For repeat parts, a custom work plate with the fixture holes saves set-up time. Check how the positioner carries the welding current: a rotary ground on the table gives the current a clean path, so it does not arc through the bearings and gears. Connect the welding ground where the maker specifies.

Sizing turning rolls and pipe supports

Turning rolls are rated by the weight per set — one drive and one idler section — and by the range of diameters the wheels can carry. Add the weight of the part and any fixtures, and leave a margin for out-of-round shells and off-centre nozzles. Very long work can rest on two idler sections beside the drive. For fit-up, cutting and welding of pipe spools, pipe stands with roller heads, folding pipe jacks and outboard supports hold the pipe at a set height while it turns; check the load rating, the pipe diameter and the height range of each stand.

Power and set-up

The premium-line WP positioners run on single-phase 220 V, so no three-phase supply is needed, and they come with a foot pedal; the value-line positioners and turning rolls are supplied for the power in your shop. Bolt a positioner to a bench, stand or floor at a comfortable working height, with room around it for the part to tilt.
