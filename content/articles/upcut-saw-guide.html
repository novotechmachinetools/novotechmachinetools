Upcut, double-head or top-down saw?

  • Upcut saw. The blade sits under the table and rises through a profile that is already clamped from above and from the side. The blade is covered until it cuts, the profile cannot move, and the cut is square and nearly burr-free. It cuts one end at a time, is flexible and inexpensive, and is enough for a few dozen windows a shift.
  • Double-head saw. It cuts both ends at once to a length typed in or read from a barcode, with both heads tilting to 45°. It roughly doubles a shop's output and takes measuring errors out of the job.
  • Top-down saws. Overhead and bench miter saws cut from above. They suit odd angles and small profiles.
  • Glazing bead saws. They cut the beads that hold the glass, to length and angle.

Many shops start with an upcut saw. They add a double-head saw for frames when output grows, and keep the upcut for mullions and odd cuts.

Blade size and the profile chart

The blade diameter decides the largest profile the saw can cut. Each saw has a chart of profile height × width at 90° and at 45°. At 45° the capacity is smaller, so check your largest profile at the angle you cut it. Most window and door profiles fit a Ø17-11/16″ (450 mm) or Ø21-5/8″ (550 mm) blade, while curtain-wall and large door profiles need Ø23-5/8″ (600 mm) or more. Look at the clamps as well: horizontal and vertical pneumatic clamps must reach your profile shapes without crushing them, and on shutter boxes and odd shapes the clamps are made to match the profile.

Aluminum or PVC: blades, feed and cooling

  • Blades. Both materials are cut with carbide-tipped blades, but the teeth are made differently. Aluminum blades have a negative rake so the teeth do not grab the profile, and PVC blades are ground for a clean cut without melting. Fit the blade made for the material you cut most.
  • Feed. The blade must rise at a steady, controlled rate, especially in aluminum. A hydro-pneumatic feed, or a hydro-check on an air feed, does this and keeps the cut clean.
  • Cooling and chips. Aluminum needs spray or mist lubrication of the blade, so chips do not weld to the teeth. PVC is cut dry, and its light chips cling by static, so the saw needs a chip extractor.

A saw that cuts both materials should have adjustable feed, a cooling device and good extraction.

Accuracy and measuring

A window frame is only as square as its four cuts. Look for a turntable with positive stops at the common angles, and a scale you can read to a fraction of a degree. Repeat lengths need a length stop, or better, a digital measuring conveyor that positions the profile to the length typed in. On a double-head saw the moving head is positioned by a servo or encoder, and the cutting list can be read from a barcode or sent from the office software. For a shop making more than a few windows a day, a measuring conveyor in front of an upcut saw is the cheapest way to more output and fewer mistakes.

Air, power and installation

Upcut saws need compressed air for the clamps and the feed, at 87 – 116 psi (6 – 8 bar), dry and filtered. They also need a chip extractor for PVC and room for the full profile length on the infeed and outfeed conveyors.
