What does a CNC wire bender do?

A CNC wire bending machine turns wire from the coil into a finished part in one pass:

  1. A decoiler pays off the wire.
  2. A straightener takes out the curve of the coil.
  3. A servo feeder pushes the programmed length through the bending head.
  4. The head bends, rotates to the next plane and bends again.
  5. A cutter drops the finished part.

Wire baskets and display racks, shelf supports, chair, sofa and bed frames, handles, hooks, springs and clips, and seat and exhaust-hanger wire are all made this way, with no jigs and no bending by hand. A part that takes a skilled worker several minutes on a hand bender comes off the machine in seconds, and every part is the same.

Wire size, material and speed

Choose the machine by the wire diameter range and the strength of the wire. On the CSM machines, the WB 10-A takes Ø5/32″ to Ø3/8″ (4 – 10 mm) and bends faster, for furniture, display and basket wire. The WB 14-A takes Ø1/4″ to Ø9/16″ (6 – 14 mm), for heavier frames, rod and seat and exhaust-hanger wire. Capacities are given for ordinary low-carbon wire. Hard-drawn, spring and stainless wire need more force for the same diameter, so check them against the larger machine. The cycle time of a part depends on the feed speed, the bending speed and the head rotation: on the WB machines the wire feeds at 164 ft/min and the head rotates at 440°/s.

Straightening: the key to accuracy

Every coil has its own curve and spring-back, and wire from different lots behaves differently. A two-plane straightener (rollers in two directions) takes the coil set out before the wire is bent. If it is not set right, the part comes out twisted or the bends drift out of plane. Look for straightener rollers that are easy to adjust and read, a decoiler that pays off smoothly without jerking the wire (motorised on heavy coils), and a servo feeder that grips the wire firmly enough to hold the length without slipping. Spring-back compensation in the control corrects the angles once they have been measured on the first part.

2D or 3D, programming and change-over

A 2D bender bends in one plane only. On a 3D bender the bending head rotates around the wire under servo control, so the next bend can be made in any plane and three-dimensional parts come off in one cycle without the operator turning the wire. Parts are programmed on the control as lengths, bend angles and rotation angles. The control shows the part in 3D, checks that the growing part does not hit the machine, and stores the program for the next batch. A new part takes minutes to program and no fixtures to build, which is what makes small batches of many different parts economical.

Lines, safety and installation

A wire bender can be the first machine of a line. With an end former, for example, the WB 14-A and a CSM EF50 make complete exhaust hangers: the wire is bent and cut, and the ends are formed. Plan how finished parts are collected: a chute or conveyor, and a bin for each part. The bending area must be guarded, because the part swings fast as it is bent.
