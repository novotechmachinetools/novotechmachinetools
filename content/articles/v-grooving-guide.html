Why groove a sheet before bending it?

On a press brake, a sheet bends around the punch tip and takes an inside radius set by the V-die, in practice about the sheet thickness or more. The outside of the bend stretches, so brushed and mirror stainless loses its finish at the corner, pre-painted sheet can crack, and the corner looks soft. A V-grooving machine planes a V-shaped channel along the bend line first, leaving only a thin web of metal. The sheet then folds about the groove to a sharp outside corner with almost no stretch, very little springback and a fraction of the force.

That is how these are made:

  • stainless elevator cabins and doors
  • column cladding and architectural trim
  • channel letters and sign boxes
  • store fixtures and appliance fronts
  • high-end furniture

It is also how a shop bends a long or thick panel on a small press brake, or even by hand.

Horizontal or vertical machine?

On a horizontal V-grooving machine the sheet lies flat on a table, held by clamps along its edge, and a gantry carries the grooving head along it. Tables run from 10.5 to 20 ft long and 49 or 59″ wide. It is the faster machine for long, thin panels and the one most sign, cladding and elevator shops choose.

On a vertical machine the sheet stands against a fixed beam, held by hydraulic clamps, and is fed from the rear. The bed cannot sag, so it grooves heavier plate (to 1/4″), and it needs about half the floor space. The trade-off is a shorter maximum length, about 13 ft.

Choose by your longest panel and your thickest sheet. If both fit either machine, the horizontal machine is usually quicker to load and unload for thin sheet.

Groove depth, angle and spacing

  • Depth. For a 90° bend the groove is usually cut to 60 – 70 % of the thickness, leaving a web of 30 – 40 %. The thinner the web, the sharper the corner and the weaker the joint. Decorative panels can be cut deeper; parts that carry load need more web left, or reinforcement inside the corner.
  • Angle. The included angle of the groove matches the angle the sheet turns through: a 90° groove for a square corner, a 45° groove for a 135° corner. If the groove is narrower than the bend, its walls meet before the bend is finished.
  • Passes. The grooving head carries several tools and takes the groove in several light cuts, roughing first and finishing last, for a clean V without tearing. Thicker sheet and harder material need more passes.
  • Edge distance and spacing. Keep the first groove at least 5/16″ from the edge of the sheet. Grooves can be programmed as single positions or as a pitch and a count.

Bending a grooved panel

A grooved panel folds on a press brake with very little tonnage, using a sharp punch and a small V. A grooved 1/4″ plate bends on a 40-ton brake. Long panels can also be folded on a folding machine or by hand along a straight edge. Groove from the back of the sheet and leave the protective film on the face, so the visible side is never touched. The corner is the weakest point of the part, so for load-carrying parts add a weld, an adhesive bead or a stiffener inside the corner.

What to compare before you buy

  • Sheet size. The longest and widest sheet you groove, and the thickest, in each material.
  • Head. How many tools the head carries, and whether the axes run on servo motors and ball screws. On NovoTech's machines the head has four tools on servo ball screws, with ± 0.01 mm positioning.
  • Clamping. Clamps must hold the sheet without marking it. Hydraulic clamping is stiffer for heavy sheet; pneumatic clamps are quick for thin sheet.
  • Speed. The cutting and return speeds, and how chips are cleared from the table.
  • Control. One that runs in inches and millimetres and stores programs.
  • Running costs. The cost of the cutting inserts and how often they are changed.
  • Site. Floor space, and power: 600 V three-phase with a CSA-approved panel as standard, or 208, 240 and 480 V on request.
