When does laser welding beat TIG and MIG?

A fiber laser welder melts a narrow, deep seam with a focused beam. Compared with TIG on thin and medium sheet it is several times faster, puts a fraction of the heat into the part and leaves a seam that usually needs no grinding. Boxes, cabinets, railings and enclosures stay flat. It also welds galvanized steel, aluminum and parts of different thicknesses that TIG struggles with, and an operator with little welding experience can make clean seams after a day of training. It is at its best on stainless, mild steel and aluminum sheet up to about 1/4″, especially where appearance and distortion matter. On heavy plate MIG and submerged-arc welding stay the workhorses, though high-power robotic laser cells reach thicker joints.

What power do I need?

Power sets the penetration and the speed. As a guide for handheld welders:

  • 0.8 – 1.5 kW. Thin sheet, roughly 20 – 14 ga stainless and mild steel.
  • 2 kW. Sheet to about 1/8″, and faster welding of thin sheet.
  • 3 kW. Joints up to about 9/32″ in mild steel in one pass, and thicker aluminum.

Aluminum and copper reflect more of the beam and conduct heat away faster, so they need more power than steel of the same thickness. Choose the power for your thickest regular joint, with some margin, and remember that more power also welds the thin parts faster.

Portable air-cooled or production water-cooled?

An air-cooled portable welder, such as the S series at 0.8 – 1.5 kW, needs no chiller, weighs about 100 – 120 lb on wheels and plugs into a 220 V single-phase outlet. It is the machine to move between jobs or take to a site. A water-cooled production welder, such as the HWM at 1.5 – 3 kW, has a chiller for continuous duty, a long cable, a wire feeder and a head that welds, cleans the weld, cuts sheet and seam-welds. It is the machine for a station that welds all day. A wire feeder adds filler for gaps and fillet welds. Without it, the laser welds butt and lap joints that fit closely.

Laser safety in the shop

A handheld laser welder is a Class 4 laser. Its invisible beam and reflections can injure the eyes and skin of the operator and of anyone nearby. Set up a controlled area: a welding booth or screens with laser-protective panels, warning signs and interlocks. Give the operator laser-safety eyewear rated for the 1,070 nm wavelength, laser-resistant gloves and clothing, and fume extraction, as for any welding. The welders fire only when the safety clamp is on the workpiece. Train every operator before the first weld; NovoTech includes the training with the installation.

Robots, cobots and cladding

When the same joint is welded hundreds of times a day (tanks, frames, boxes, exhaust and furniture), a robotic laser cell holds the seam in position on a positioner and welds it the same way every time, at 1.5 to 20 kW. A collaborative robot (cobot) with a MIG torch is the simplest entry to automation: it is programmed by guiding it by hand over the part. Laser cladding uses the same sources to fuse a wear- or corrosion-resistant alloy onto worn shafts, rods, rolls and dies, with less distortion than overlay welding.
