What size arbor press do I need?

An arbor press is a hand press: a lever turns a pinion that drives a toothed ram down with many times the force of the hand. It presses bearings, bushings and pins in and out, broaches keyways, stakes and rivets, all with a feel for the work that no powered press gives. Choose it by force and by the space under the ram:

  • 1 – 2 tons. Small bearings, pins, dowels and staking.
  • 3 – 5 tons. Most automotive and machine bearings, and broaching.
  • 6 – 8 tons. Larger bearings and gears, with up to about 17-3/4″ of height and 10″ of diameter between the ram and the base.

Check the throat (the distance from the ram to the frame) and the maximum height under the ram for your largest part. A stepped base plate with slots of several widths supports shafts and housings close to the work. For more force, move up to a hydraulic shop press.

Standard, ratchet or rack-and-pinion?

On a standard arbor press the lever turns through a limited arc, so a long stroke takes several strokes of the lever. A ratchet arbor press lets the lever be reset to the best position without the ram moving, so the operator always presses with the most leverage. That makes it easier for long strokes and repeated work. Compact rack-and-pinion presses are made for fast, repeated small-part work on the bench, such as assembly and staking.

Bench presses for sheet-metal tooling

A bench press with standard tooling is a small sheet-metal shop in itself. It punches round, square and oblong holes, punches and flares or beads holes for stiffness and hoses, cuts louvers, notches corners, bends short flanges and shears light sheet. The hydraulic version takes heavier and repeated work, and a combination press holds several tools at once for production. In an HVAC or sheet-metal shop it does the small jobs a turret punch or a press brake would be wasted on.

Turret punch presses and orbital riveters

A manual turret punch press carries a set of punches and dies on a rotating turret, so the operator turns to the next size instead of changing tools. It is the economical way to punch a variety of holes in small batches of sheet. An orbital riveter tilts its riveting head a few degrees and rolls it around the rivet end, forming the head gradually with a fraction of the force a straight punch would need. The part does not deform, the head is smooth and the machine is quiet: the method for hinges, chain links, brake parts, appliances and hardware.

Using a hand press well

Bolt the press to a solid bench or stand at elbow height. Support the part squarely, as close to the press point as the base allows, and press parts straight. A bearing pressed at an angle damages its seat. Press only on the ring you are fitting: on the inner ring for a shaft and on the outer ring for a housing. Wear eye protection, because hardened parts can crack. Tell us the parts you press and their sizes, and we will recommend the press and the tooling.
