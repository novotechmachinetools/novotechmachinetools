How does an angle roll work?

An angle roll, also called a section bender, profile bender, ring roller or tube roller, bends a bar, angle, channel, pipe, tube or beam by passing it between three rolls. Two rolls drive the section and hold it, and the third is moved in to push it into a curve. Each pass takes the section to a tighter radius, until the arc or ring is finished. A curved handrail, a tank stiffener ring, a rolled flange or an arched truss is made the same way. Because the rolls must grip the section to bend it, the ends of the bar stay straight for a short length. Machines whose outer rolls move independently pre-bend the ends and leave a shorter straight end, and for a full ring you can leave extra length and cut it off.

Why the section matters more than the size

The force an angle roll needs depends on the shape and orientation of the section, not only on its size. A flat bar rolled the easy way (flat) takes far less force than the same bar rolled on edge (the hard way). An angle rolled leg-out is different from leg-in, and a channel flange-in is different from flange-out. That is why every capacity chart lists each profile and position separately, with the largest section and the smallest diameter it can be rolled to. Charts are for mild steel. Stainless, aluminum alloys that are hard to bend and high-strength sections usually need the next model up, and a tighter diameter in the same section also needs a bigger machine. For daily production, choose a machine one size above your largest section: the minimum diameter shrinks and the rolls and shafts last longer.

Twist, distortion and roll sets

Asymmetric sections such as angle and channel tend to twist as they bend, and tube tends to flatten. Three things control it:

  • Lateral guide rolls. Manual or hydraulic, they keep angle and channel square to the rolls.
  • Roll sets. A set shaped for the section supports its walls: pipe rolls for each pipe size, and rolls for angle, channel and special profiles.
  • Delrin or nylon rolls. They roll polished stainless and aluminum without marking.

A standard roll set comes with the machine for flat bar, square bar and similar sections. Rolls for your pipe, tube and profiles are made to your section drawing. Order them with the machine, so the first job is not waiting for tooling.

Horizontal or vertical, NC and spirals

Many angle rolls work in both positions:

  • Vertical. It takes less floor space and suits short pieces and rings.
  • Horizontal. It lets long bars, pipe and beams run out over the floor on supports.

Digital readouts show the position of the bending roll, so a radius can be repeated. An NC control stores the roll positions for each pass and repeats the whole bend for series parts. With a spiral attachment, the machine also rolls spiral staircases, stringers and heating coils, and a dedicated coiling machine rolls variable-pitch coils automatically.
