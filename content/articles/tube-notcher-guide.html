Why cope tube ends?

Wherever one tube meets another in a T or at an angle, the end of the tube must be coped (notched) to the curve of the tube it joins. Handrail posts, roll cages, furniture and bike frames, trailers and gates are all built this way. A good cope fits with a gap of no more than about 1/16″, so the weld closes in one pass, uses less filler, distorts the frame less and looks right. Cut by hand with a grinder, a cope takes minutes and rarely fits the first time. A tube notcher cuts it in seconds, square or at an angle, and the same every time.

Belt, hole-saw or end-mill notcher?

  • Belt notchers. They grind the saddle on an abrasive belt wrapped around a drum the size of the mating tube. They are fast, work on any material, including stainless and aluminum, and leave a clean, burr-free edge. The drum is changed in seconds for the next size, and the same machine grinds flat and chamfers ends.
  • Hole-saw notchers. They drive a bi-metal hole saw of the mating tube's size through a gearbox, or from a drill or a drill press. The machine is inexpensive and the angle is easy to set, but the cut is slower, the saws dull quickly on stainless, and the edge needs deburring.
  • End-mill notchers. They cut the saddle with an end mill: fast and quiet, with no coolant, no dust, and a precise fit. They are the choice for frequent notching of small and medium tube.

For very large pipe, or when a part also needs holes and slots, a CNC plasma pipe cutter or a tube laser does the coping as part of the cut.

Size, angle and set-up

Choose the notcher by the range of tube you cope (for example 11/16″ to 3-1/2″ OD on the PN 17/90) and the angles you need. Most notchers cope from 90° down to 30° – 50°. Check that a drum, hole saw or end mill is available for every mating tube size, and that the vise holds round and square tube without marking it. An angle scale with clear degree markings, a positive lock and a stop for repeat lengths make batch work fast. If you build frames in square tube as well, a belt notcher that also grinds flat and on a contact wheel covers both.

Belts, dust and safety

Use coarse belts (36 – 60 grit) for fast stock removal on steel, and ceramic or zirconia belts for stainless, which last several times longer than standard aluminum-oxide belts. Belt notchers make grinding dust and sparks, so connect them to a dust collector and keep the area clear of anything that burns. Never grind aluminum and steel into the same collector: the mixed dust is a fire and explosion hazard. Hole-saw and end-mill notchers make chips instead of dust, but they need a guard and a vise that holds the tube firmly. The motorised notchers run on 600 V three-phase with a CSA-approved panel (208, 240 or 480 V on request), and the bench and drill-driven notchers need no power of their own.
