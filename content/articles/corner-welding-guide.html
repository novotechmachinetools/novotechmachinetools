What does a corner welding machine do?

Boxes, cabinets, trays, enclosures and hoods bent from sheet have to be welded at their corners. By hand, every corner depends on the welder: the speed varies, the heat builds up, thin sheet warps and the weld then has to be ground and dressed. A corner welding machine carries the TIG or plasma torch along a guide at a set speed, while the part is held square against copper blocks that draw off the heat. The operator places the part, clamps it with a button and presses start, then prepares the next part while the machine welds. Every corner comes out straight, even and the same, and needs less grinding afterwards.

Why the copper blocks matter

The part is pressed from inside against copper blocks: an external guide and an internal unit matched to the corner height. They hold the corner square while it is welded, which matters more than anything for a good-looking box. They also draw the heat away, so thin sheet does not warp and stainless does not discolour as much. On stainless, a cooling unit for the copper keeps the colour down and saves pickling and polishing. Ask for it if you weld stainless every day.

Which parts fit?

Check three sizes against your parts: the smallest and largest box (on the CWS from 3-15/16 × 3-15/16″ to 59 × 59″), the corner height (up to 7-7/8″ on the CWS 200 and 11-13/16″ on the CWS 300), and the material and thickness. Mild steel, stainless and aluminum are all welded. Bent corners with a small gap and a consistent bend radius weld best, so good press brake work upstream pays off at the welder.

TIG or plasma, and the power source

TIG is the usual choice for thin sheet and for the cleanest-looking welds on stainless and aluminum. Plasma welding is faster, with deeper penetration and a more focused arc, and suits longer production runs. The machine carries the torch of a TIG or plasma power source on its guide, with travel speeds from 0 to 118 in/min. We quote it with a matching power source, or connect one you already have. Tell us the material, thickness and corner you weld, and we will set up the process.

Installation

The corner welder stands on a level floor near the press brake, with shielding gas and the welding power source beside it, and fume extraction as for any welding. It runs on 600 V three-phase with a CSA-approved panel (208, 240 or 480 V on request). NovoTech installs it, sets up the process on your first parts and trains your operators. A corner grinder next to it finishes the welded corners to a flush or radiused edge for painting or polishing.
