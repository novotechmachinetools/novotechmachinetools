How does a circle cutting machine work?

The sheet is clamped at its centre on a spindle and turned, by hand or by motor, while two rotary blades shear a circle at the radius set on the arm. A second pass at a smaller radius cuts the inside diameter and leaves a ring. The result is a round blank or ring with a clean sheared edge, no heat and no dross, cut in seconds. Circle shears make blanks for tank heads, fan and blower housings, lids, flanges, gaskets, duct fittings and spun parts.

A laser or plasma table also cuts circles, but more slowly and at a higher cost per part. A circle shear is the economical machine when you cut many round blanks or rings. For shapes other than circles, the laser or plasma remains the right tool.

Which size and thickness?

Choose the machine by three numbers:

  • Largest diameter. Up to Ø78.7″ (2 m) on the IDK-5, and to about Ø15 ft on the SDK tank-head machines.
  • Smallest diameter. From about Ø4.3″ on the circle shears.
  • Thickest sheet. Rated in mild steel, from 14 ga on the smaller IDK models to 3/16″ on the hydraulic IDK-5. Stainless steel reduces the capacity by about one gauge.

Size the machine for the thickest material at the largest diameter you cut, not for the occasional small blank.

Clamping and drive

The centre clamp holds the sheet on the spindle while it turns, and it decides how fast the machine works. A hand-screw clamp is fine for a few parts at a time. A pneumatic clamp on shop air, or a hydraulic clamp on the heavy machines, is fast and consistent for production. A motorised blade drive turns the sheet at a steady speed for a smooth, round edge. Check how quickly the radius is set and read on the arm: a clear scale and quick locking save time on every new size.

Tank heads: cutting and flanging on one machine

A flat or dished tank head is first cut round, then its edge is turned up (flanged) to a radius between top and bottom flanging rolls. On a tank-head cutting and flanging machine, such as the SDK 6 and SDK 8, the same head first cuts the disc from the square plate with rotary blades and then flanges it, without moving the part. Hydraulic side supports hold large heads, and a touchscreen runs the cycle. These machines take plate up to 5/16″ and heads up to about Ø15 ft. An elliptical version (EFM 6) does the same for oval heads. The flange radius comes from the roll set, so tell us the head drawing with its radius when you ask for a quote.
