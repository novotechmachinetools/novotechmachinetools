What does a corner notcher do?

Before a sheet is folded into a box, pan, tray or enclosure, a square or V-shaped piece has to come out of each corner, so the four sides can fold up without colliding. Cut by hand with snips or a grinder, that takes minutes per corner and is never the same twice. A corner notcher cuts it in one stroke with two shear blades set at 90° (or at any angle on a variable-angle machine). Stops on the table put every notch in the same place.

The rectangular notching station of an ironworker cuts a slot into the edge of bar and plate. A corner notcher is made for large sheets: they lie flat on its table against the gauges, and it cuts at 20 – 30 strokes a minute. Sheet-metal shops that fold boxes every day want the notcher. Structural shops are usually served by the ironworker.

Fixed 90° or variable angle?

Boxes, pans, trays and electrical enclosures have 90° corners, and a fixed 90° notcher cuts them fastest, with the least to set. Hoppers, chutes, cones, duct transitions and any part whose sides are not square need a variable-angle notcher, whose blades can be set to any angle within their range (30° to 140° on the HNM 6V). Some shops start with a fixed notcher and add a variable one when the work calls for it.

Capacity: thickness, notch size and material

  • Thickness. Notchers are rated in mild steel, from about 1/8″ on pneumatic notchers to 1/4″ on the heavy hydraulic machines. Stainless takes about half of that: 14 ga on a 3/16″ notcher and 11 ga on a 1/4″ notcher. Aluminum takes up to the mild-steel rating.
  • Notch size. The blade length sets the largest notch in one stroke. Measure your deepest flange and add the material thickness.
  • Blade gap. The gap between the blades must grow with the thickness. A machine with automatic gap adjustment changes from thin sheet to plate without resetting the blades, and both stay clean-cut.
  • Drive. Hydraulic notchers cut 3/16 – 1/4″ at production speed. Pneumatic notchers run on shop air for sheet to about 1/8″ — quieter, with no hydraulic oil, for the shop that notches now and then.

Getting the notch right

Cut the notch to the flange height plus about one material thickness, so the folded sides meet at the corner without crashing into each other or bulging. Set the stops for the flange size and cut all four corners of a batch before folding. For welded corners, a slightly smaller notch leaves a tight seam to weld. For corners that are left open or riveted, a slightly larger notch keeps the corner clean. Keep the blades sharp and correctly gapped: a dull blade leaves a burr that shows on the finished box, and the blades are resharpened and shimmed back to size.

Safety and installation

Every powered notcher needs a guard in front of the blades and a sheet hold-down, and is worked by a foot pedal so both hands stay on the sheet, clear of the blades. The machines are compact, stand on a level floor, and run on 600 V three-phase with a CSA-approved panel (208, 240 or 480 V on request). Pneumatic models run on shop air.
