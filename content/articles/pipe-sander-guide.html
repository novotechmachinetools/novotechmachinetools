Which pipe sander for which tube?

Pipe sanders run abrasive belts or brushes over round or square tube to remove rust, scale and weld discolouration, give stainless a brushed satin finish, or prepare tube for paint, plating or mirror polishing. The kind of tube decides the machine:

Straight or bent tube?

A centreless sander drives straight tube or bar through its heads between a contact wheel and a work support, for the most even finish at the highest speed. It cannot finish a bend. An orbital sander moves its belts around the tube as the tube is pushed through the head, so elbows, frames and bent handrail are finished after bending, and oval tube too. The BT 114-S head and feeder follow bends up to 45°. If you finish both straight and bent tube, decide which makes up most of your work, or plan two machines.

Finish and grit

The finish follows the belt. A coarse grit (40 – 80) removes rust, scale and weld colour. 180 – 240 grit gives the brushed satin finish of architectural stainless handrail. Finer and non-woven belts prepare the tube for mirror polishing. Work through the grits in steps. On a two-head or two-belt machine, fit a cutting belt and a finishing belt so one pass does both. To match an existing finish, send us a sample: the grit and the belt type decide whether new and old tube look the same.

Stainless, dust and installation

Keep the belts, brushes and supports that touch stainless for stainless only. Iron particles from carbon-steel work embed in the surface and show up later as rust spots. Pipe sanders make fine grinding dust, so connect them to a dust collector, and never run aluminum and steel dust into the same dry collector. Roller stands on the infeed and outfeed support long tube. The machines run on 600 V three-phase with a CSA-approved panel (208, 240 or 480 V on request).
