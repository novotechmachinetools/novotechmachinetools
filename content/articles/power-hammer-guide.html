Which hammer does what?

Auto body and hot-rod builders, restoration shops, motorcycle and aircraft fabricators shape and finish sheet with hammers, and blacksmiths forge hot bar under them. Each kind does one job best:

  • Pneumatic planishing hammer. Fast, light blows between a top die and a crowned lower anvil smooth a panel and take out the marks of earlier shaping. It runs on shop air.
  • Electric power hammer. Heavier blows at a variable speed. With shaping dies it raises, stretches, shrinks and beads the panel, so it shapes as well as planishes.
  • Roller former. A motorized pair of patterned rolls that embosses a design, such as diamonds, into sheet for decorative panels.
  • Air forging hammer. A self-contained hammer for hot forging: drawing out, upsetting, punching, chiselling and forge welding bar and billet.

Planishing hammer or power hammer?

A pneumatic planishing hammer delivers many light blows a second. It is the tool to finish a panel and to make small corrections, and it is light, quiet and simple. An electric power hammer, with variable speed and more force, shapes as well as planishes: with shaping dies it shrinks, stretches and raises panels that would take hours by hand. Many shops have both, the power hammer to shape and the planishing hammer to finish.

Choosing a planishing or power hammer

  • Throat depth. The distance from the dies to the back of the frame limits how far into a panel you can work. A 12″ throat suits brackets, patch panels and motorcycle parts, 20 – 24″ most car panels, and 30″ reaches the middle of hoods, roofs and large skins.
  • Frame stiffness. The frame must not spring open under the blows, or the dies lose their setting and the panel is marked. A heavy welded C-frame matters more than any other feature.
  • Dies and anvils. A set of lower anvils from flat to a tight radius lets you choose how fast and where the panel moves. Check that the dies change quickly and that shaping, shrinking and beading dies are available.
  • Capacity. Planishing hammers take 18 – 16 ga steel, power hammers 16 ga steel and 12 ga aluminum. Aluminum moves more easily than steel of the same thickness.
  • Air or power. A planishing hammer needs clean, dry shop air at the pressure on its plate, typically 50 – 120 psi, through a filter, regulator and lubricator. A power hammer needs electric power and a foot pedal to set the blow rate.

Air forging hammers

An air forging hammer is sized by the weight of its ram, which sets the size of the stock it can forge. A 35 – 55 lb ram suits blades, tools and ornamental work up to about 1-1/2″ square; an 88 lb ram takes 2″ square, a 165 lb ram 2-1/2″ square and Ø3-3/8″ round. The hammer is self-contained, its motor driving a compressor cylinder in the frame, so it needs no shop air. It needs a solid concrete foundation, separate from the shop floor, to absorb the blows, and the larger hammers stand on a separate anvil block.
