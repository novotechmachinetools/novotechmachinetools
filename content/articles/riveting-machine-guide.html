What an orbital riveter does

A riveting machine forms the head of a solid or semi-tubular rivet that holds two or more parts together. A press or punch sets the whole head in one blow and needs a lot of force, which can bend a thin part, loosen a hinge or crack a casting. An orbital (rolling) riveter tilts its riveting tool a few degrees and rolls it around the rivet end, so the head is formed a little at a time with about a tenth of the force. The part keeps its fit, the head comes out smooth and even, and the machine runs without the hammering noise and vibration of impact riveting. It replaces hammering, punching and hot riveting on hinges, chain links, brake shoes, appliances, hand tools and hardware, and with other tools in the spindle the machine also works as a small press.

Semi-tubular and hollow rivets need much less force than solid rivets of the same diameter, so a smaller machine often sets them. Hard materials need more: for stainless steel rivets, or for a long rivet end that has to be rolled down a long way, take the next size up. Head forms and riveting tools

The riveting tool (the peen) in the spindle gives the head its shape, and the XM riveters take 11 head forms. The common ones are the domed (round) head, the flat head, the flush head for a countersunk hole and the rolled-over head that flares a semi-tubular rivet. Order one tool for each head your parts need, plus a spare for the one you run most: the tool wears in production, and a worn tool leaves a rough head. The tool must match the rivet diameter, so a plant that sets several sizes keeps a set of tools for each.

Stroke, height and work holding

Check two dimensions against your largest part. The opening from the riveting head to the table (4.7″ on the XM-5, 8.7 to 11.8″ on the larger models) must take the part, the fixture and the rivet standing up in it. The spindle travel (0.8 to 2″) must reach the rivet end from where the tool starts. The table size decides how large a part or fixture you can lay down.

For repeat work, hold the part in a fixture with locating pins, so every rivet sits right under the tool, and set the down stop. The stop sets the head height, which makes every head the same and keeps the rivet from being over-formed and the joint from binding. On hinges and other moving joints, test a few parts until the joint turns freely before you run the batch.

Power, installation and safety

The XM-5 is compact, about 17 × 13 × 32″; the larger models are floor machines up to 87″ high that need a level spot and room to load parts from the front. The machines are supplied for 600 V three-phase with a CSA-approved panel as standard; 208, 240 and 480 V are available on request. Keep the operator's hands out of the riveting area: load the part in the fixture first, then start the cycle. For production use, ask us about guarding for your parts.
